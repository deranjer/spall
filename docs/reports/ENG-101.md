# ENG-101 (R4b, first pass) — Moving bodies in the lighting cache, incremental updates

Follows ENG-97 (`docs/reports/ENG-97.md` §6). This pass makes moving bodies and
detached debris part of the lighting (they block skylight and act as bounce
sources), and replaces the whole-cache recompute with a time-sliced sweep so a
body that moves every frame is affordable. Camera scroll, streamed-boundary
runs, low-angle sampling and end-to-end edit latency remain open (§5). Nothing
here claims a G2 gate result.

## 1. Design

**Occupancy = terrain + bodies, kept exactly.** `SkyVisibility` keeps the last
terrain grid on the CPU (`base`) and the set of cells bodies currently override
(`cell -> material`). `set_bodies` is called every frame with the posed body
cubes (`GameRenderer::set_bodies` forwards to it). Each cube marks the cells its
rotated bounding box touches (conservative: over-occlusion, never leakage, as for
terrain). The new map is diffed against the old one and only cells whose *final*
occupancy changed are uploaded:

- a cell a body **enters** takes the body's material;
- a cell a body **leaves** takes its **terrain value back** (never erased to air);
- a cell two bodies share stays occupied when only one leaves.

So old and new bounds are both updated and overlapping objects survive. A new
terrain grid (an edit) has the current bodies laid back over it.

**Time-sliced sweeps.** A change starts a *sweep* that recomputes sky visibility
and the bounce over the 128 z-slices, 16 per frame, so it completes in 8 frames
and a moving body costs 1/8 of a full recompute per frame. The first population,
a re-centre of the cache, or an environment/material/toggle change is still one
full dispatch. Requests that arrive while a sweep runs are queued for the next.
The sweep recomputes the **whole** cache, which is a superset of every cell whose
rays (24 m) could pass through a changed cell, so nothing beyond the edited
bounds is left stale; a tighter dirty-bounds cull is possible but not needed at
this cost (§5).

## 2. Evidence (measured on this machine)

RTX 4080 SUPER, D3D12, wgpu 30. `cargo test -p spall_render --test
dynamic_lighting_gpu -- --ignored --nocapture`; images in
`.local/runs/eng-101-dynamic/`.

- **A body blocks skylight and leaves no trail.** Ground under a raised slab:
  0.462 open → 0.068 shaded (settled in 9 frames) → after the body is removed the
  frame is **identical to the never-had-a-body frame** (mean |diff| 0.0000/255,
  max 0).
- **Rapid motion leaves no ghosts.** A rotated body swept 30 frames, then settled
  in 11 frames, versus a fresh render with the body at rest in its final pose:
  mean |diff| 0.0000, max 0.
- **Moving bodies are lit and light.** An emissive body moved from x = −6 to +6:
  the floor beside the old position goes 0.070 → 0.000 and beside the new one
  0.000 → 0.070, settled in 9 frames.
- **Overlap is safe.** Two overlapping slabs: 0.462 open, 0.009 with both, 0.004
  with only one left (still blocking), and the open frame restored exactly when
  both are gone. CPU tests pin the same diff logic (vacated terrain restored,
  shared cells untouched, unchanged bodies upload nothing, rotated boxes never
  shrink, bodies outside the cache add nothing).
- **Existing acceptance unchanged:** `capture_gpu` 10/10 (incl. the Vulkan
  pipeline-compile probe), `bounce_gpu` 7/7, `sky_visibility_gpu` 6/6, shadows,
  parity, viewport; workspace tests pass.
- **Cost, release, 1080p, 200 moving bodies** (`body-cost.json`): `set_bodies` +
  record + submit p50 1.69 ms (p95 2.42 ms); the per-frame slice is 0.29 ms sky
  + 0.69 ms bounce of GPU time; a sweep is 8 frames. (A debug build spends ~7 ms
  on the same CPU work: the cell diff is not optimised there.)
- **Live** (`cargo xtask play --scene playground`, debug build, ~2,200 body cubes,
  ~200 bodies moving): no errors, frame average 16.6 ms, maximum 17–24 ms, sweeps
  8 frames, shown in the report line (`lighting sweep N frames`).

## 3. Edit-to-lighting latency (not measured end to end)

From the pieces: a body's light follows its motion within one sweep, **8 frames
(~133 ms at 60 fps)**, plus up to 8 more if a sweep was already running.
Terrain edits additionally wait for the client's terrain recheck (500 ms) and
rebuild before the changed grid is sent; those stages were not timed together,
and the recorded number is therefore an estimate (recheck + rebuild + ≤16
frames), to be measured under ENG-99.

## 4. Behaviour to know about

- Lighting for moving bodies lags by up to a sweep. Sun shadows from bodies are
  unaffected (they come from the shadow maps every frame).
- Rotated bodies occupy their axis-aligned bounding box (over-occlusion, up to
  ~1.7× the volume at 45°).
- Bodies are drawn from the same cube list the client already builds each frame;
  a body larger than the cache, or outside it, contributes nothing.

## 5. Not done

(Scroll, low-angle sampling, emissive content and edit latency were taken up in
`docs/reports/ENG-102.md`; the list below is as of ENG-101.)

- Camera scroll (clipmap): the cache still re-centres with a full recompute after
  8 m of drift.
- A dirty-bounds cull of the sweep (recompute only cells within ray range of the
  change); the sliced full sweep is used instead.
- Exercising the unknown-cell policy in a streamed-world run.
- Better low-angle sampling / smoothing of the blocky bleed.
- Emissive content in the sandbox manifest; end-to-end edit latency; moving
  footage; Vulkan/non-NVIDIA measurement.
- Bodies are not distinguished by owner or persisted: the lighting cache stays
  derived and client-local.
