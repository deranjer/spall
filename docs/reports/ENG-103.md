# ENG-103 — Water feasibility prototype

Status: both the bounded Salva prototype and a separate MAC/VOF grid comparison remain isolated from the authoritative tick. The feasibility gate is **not passed**. The Salva evidence and its original failing baselines remain preserved. Latest bounded diagnosis recommends keeping the MAC candidate for a pressure-convergence investigation; do not integrate either backend.

## Changes in this pass

- The JSONL harness now separately records retained particle mass/volume, current volume outside the domain, cumulative permitted open-top outflow, swept solid penetration, first invalid crossing (type, tick, particle, position, velocity, voxel), density error, solver-call time, wrapper diagnostics, phase-end density-probe cost, boundary rebuild time, and normalized 1/60-second simulation cost.
- Density is sampled at phase end and labeled accordingly. Per-step speed, energy, crossing, and outside-domain values are accumulated through each phase. The density diagnostic is an independent absolute relative density-error estimate, not Salva's solver residual.
- Voxel changes stage a cloned `Volume`, edit, boundary capture, boundary-particle build, and overlap validation before publishing the new fluid boundary and voxel state. Rejected geometry leaves the prior voxel sample, boundary mask, and particle positions intact. Particle positions are never deleted, teleported, or clamped.
- Added an initially flat basin, separate from the two initially collapsing reservoir blocks. Tests cover intact containment, canal transfer and level difference, downstream breach momentum, canal closure, staged-edit rejection, stability measurements, and a separate upper pool over a flooded lower passage.
- A local Salva 0.10.0 source copy adds per-substep pressure/divergence iteration counts, stopping residuals, and convergence flags. Dependency version remains 0.10.0. Salva's own stage timer still returns zero because its timer clock calls are disabled upstream; external wall-clock timing around every complete `LiquidWorld::step` call is used instead. The local fork enables a same-version parallel-feature experiment.

## Declared gates and actual setup

The existing proposed feasibility allocation is 2 ms per 1/60-second fluid step at base scale. The workspace total server-tick p95 target is 12 ms. Numerical targets used for the stability fixture are: 3 simulated seconds; late window is ticks 120–179; p95 of per-tick p95 speed < 0.5 m/s; late-window kinetic energy <= initial + 1 J; surface p95 drift < 0.15 m; phase-end maximum absolute density error < 2%; and zero intact-solid crossings. Canal transfer requires a downstream particle increase and at least 0.03 m reduction in p95 water-level difference over 2 seconds. Breach momentum must exceed 0.1 kg m/s after 10 ticks. Closure must prevent downstream transfer over 30 ticks. Tunnel duration is 2 seconds, with no upper-pool particle loss, no solid crossing, and less than 1e-9 m³ outside-domain volume.

Base domain: 24 x 12 x 8 voxel cells at 0.25 m; 1,472 particles, 0.125 m spacing, 2.300000034 m³, smoothing factor 2 (kernel support 0.25 m), gravity 9.81 m/s², 1/60 s configured tick and one 1/60 s solver call. Boundary particles are sampled on each exposed voxel face with `ceil(cell_size/(2r))` samples per side and deduplicated. Floor, side, and end walls are closed; top is open; there is no water source. DFSPH defaults: min/max pressure iterations 1/50 and maximum average density residual 0.05; min/max divergence iterations 1/50 and maximum divergence error 0.1. `TimestepManager::compute_substep` currently returns the whole supplied step: automatic CFL subdivision is disabled. Fixed substeps in these runs are explicit wrapper calls at equal smaller timesteps.

The scale-2 domain is 48 x 24 x 16, with 13,696 particles and 21.400000319 m³. Release timings are local to the host used for this run; CPU model was not independently verified. Process memory is the maximum working set sampled every 50 ms, not an OS-guaranteed peak. The particle-array figure is a separate lower bound and excludes Salva spatial/contact/solver work buffers.

## Bounded MAC/VOF comparison

The separate `grid_mac` backend is documented before its implementation in
[`ENG-103-grid-method.md`](ENG-103-grid-method.md). It remains outside the
authoritative tick. It stores cell-centered liquid fraction and pressure with
staggered face velocities, projects with matrix-free preconditioned CG, and
transports water through bounded shared-face FCT fluxes. Fixed outer ticks use
equal CFL/gravity-limited substeps; if the configured maximum of eight is
insufficient, the call errors without advancing. All measured substeps are
included in timings. Solid cells cannot contain fraction, and solid-normal
velocity is zero. The same-domain geometry edit is staged and only committed
after candidate boundary validation.

Unlike particle Salva, this grid has no particle coordinates outside its finite
domain: current out-of-domain volume is zero by representation. Open-top runs
record permitted boundary outflow explicitly in the global volume balance;
sealed runs have zero permitted outflow. This does not imply that regional
pool volumes stay fixed.

The runner records the actual cell/grid/timestep/solver configuration in each
JSONL file. `fluid_update_*` is the `step` wall time including every internal
substep. `solver_cost_per_1_60_simulated_second_ms` scales the mean solver call
time to 1/60 second of simulated time. `cost_per_1_60_simulated_second_ms`
adds the runner's separately measured diagnostics. `boundary_update_ms_last`
measures one staged edit (0 for unedited runs); process working set is sampled
every 10 ms by PowerShell and is not an OS-guaranteed peak. Grid allocated
bytes include its resident arrays, not the process/runtime. The host CPU was
identified from the Windows registry as AMD Ryzen 7 9700X (16 logical
processors); OS hardware inventory could not provide physical RAM, so total
host RAM is unknown. Density error is not applicable to this fractional grid
state; its relevant checks are bounded fraction, volume/mass balance, pressure
residual, and post-projection divergence.

Targets were declared before the final run: 2 ms solver cost per 1/60-second
fluid interval at base scale; 3 seconds of initially level basin water with
late-window max face speed <0.5 m/s, kinetic energy increase <=1 J, and surface
p95 drift <0.15 m; canal transfer >=0.03 m³ and >=0.03 m level-difference
reduction over 2 seconds; breach momentum >0.1 kg m/s and discharge

wall-top flux; a changed regional volume alone does not prove a solid crossing.

### Initial grid comparison disposition (superseded below)

The initial dense MAC implementation meets boundedness, analytical projection,
conservation, canal, breach, and short closure checks, but misses the base
2 ms allocation in every measured base fluid case and is roughly 24–37x over
that budget at scale 2. Its full-basin energy/speed gate fails, and scale-2
tunnel regional containment remains unresolved. **Decision: do not continue
this MAC implementation as the candidate solver. Compare one alternative
bounded 3D grid solver against these same fixtures and gates.** Salva also
remains unfit for promotion because its preserved tunnel baseline crosses an
intact roof and scale 2 is far outside budget. Neither backend is ready for
boats, networking, or production integration.

velocity in liquid cells; the former maximum single face component is not used
as the stability result. Kinetic energy uses the staggered face velocities
weighted by adjacent liquid fractions in each face's dual volume. Gravitational

### Decision

The corrected diagnostics remove the scale-2 tunnel containment failure; the
measured volume loss there was redistribution into an omitted cavity row, not
an intact-roof crossing. Basin sensitivity is now multi-step and at equal
physical duration. It shows improvement in energy and surface drift but does
not meet the late-speed target. The optimized base sealed/canal/breach cases

pressure projection dominates its runtime. **Keep this candidate for one
bounded investigation of pressure-solver convergence/iteration cost.** This
identified limit is not yet an unreasonable repair cost or a reason to replace
the solver. Production integration, boats, and networking remain blocked.

## Pressure cost and basin residual-motion follow-up (2026-09-27)

This bounded pass preserves Salva and the initial MAC evidence above. No water

### Timing harness correction and reproducible workloads

The earlier cost normalization multiplied mean outer-tick cost by `60 * dt`.
That is backwards for fixed-step normalization. The corrected factor is
`(1/60) / dt`: a 120 Hz outer tick is multiplied by two because two outer
steps advance each 1/60-second interval. The old half-timestep value of
0.917 ms is superseded; equal-duration final captures measure 1.866 ms for

### Pressure system and targeted cost change

The matrix-free seven-point pressure operator has reciprocal `-1/h^2`
off-diagonals and the matching positive face contributions on its diagonal.
Atmospheric free-surface faces add a positive diagonal anchor. Thus each
atmospherically anchored component is symmetric positive definite; enclosed
all-Neumann components are positive semidefinite with one constant null mode
per component. Their compatible right-hand sides and Krylov vectors are
projected onto the zero-mean subspace. The diagonal Jacobi preconditioner has
positive entries and is symmetric positive on that subspace. A focused test
checks symmetry and positivity for a closed three-cell component; the
two-cell analytical pressure/nullspace test still passes.

The targeted change removed redundant per-iteration zero-mean projections
of pressure and residual vectors. They start compatible and zero-mean, and
remain so under `A p` and the compatible right-hand side; preconditioned `z`
and search direction `d` still receive explicit projections. Pressure gets
one final gauge projection before publication. Active-index reductions avoid
dry cells, and a committed geometry boundary edit clears the pressure warm
start and liquid-set history. The geometry invalidation path has a focused test.

The uninstrumented scale-2 tunnel pressure time fell from 52.052 to
36.797 ms per tick (29.3%); total solver time fell from 63.597 to 47.979 ms
per tick. Normalized all-in cost fell from 63.955 to 48.388 ms per 1/60
simulated second (24.3%). Pressure iterations remained unchanged, confirming
this is less work per Krylov iteration rather than better convergence. A
separate detailed capture recorded every pressure substep so the timers do
not contaminate this main comparison. It measured per substep averages of
12.25 ms matrix applications, 2.13 ms Jacobi application, 20.37 ms vector and
nullspace operations, and 34.88 ms total Krylov work; remaining projection
setup, face correction, and divergence checks account for the balance.
Velocity advection (4.44 ms/tick) and fraction transport (6.65 ms/tick) are
the next measured costs outside pressure, followed by about 0.061 ms/tick of
boundary handling. This scale-2 workload remains about 24 times over the
proposed 2 ms average allocation; the improvement does not establish
scalability.

The detailed run contains 124 substep records with residual histories,
component sizes and anchors, active cells, entering/leaving wet cells,
nonzero warm-start counts, true residual recomputations, and separate
projection timers. Residuals are L2 norms over active-cell pressure-equation
rows, in Pa/m^2. The convergence threshold is
`max(abs_tol, rel_tol * initial_L2)`, with a fixed absolute floor of
1e-8 Pa/m^2 in that norm to handle near-zero right-hand sides. At the
representative middle substep, there is one 13,828-cell enclosed component,
all cells reuse nonzero previous pressure, the warm-start initial residual
is 0.001824 of the RHS norm, and the final true residual is 2.62e-5 below
the 2.89e-5 threshold. The late sample’s ratio is 0.000337. The first
substep is un-warmed, with 1,627 wet cells across three atmospheric
components. Independently recomputed residuals were below threshold on all
124 substeps; the maximum recursive-versus-recomputed absolute difference
was 1.19e-8. The maximum residual ratio was 0.99941, so reported convergence
agreed with the recomputation and post-projection divergence.

## Bounded IC(0) pressure comparison (2026-09-27)

Implemented selectable IC(0) preconditioning beside the retained Jacobi-PCG
baseline. `MacGridWorld` stays Jacobi by default; the scenario runner accepts
`--preconditioner jacobi|ic0`. Both algorithms solve the same matrix-free
seven-point pressure operator, with no dependency changes. IC(0) uses
deterministic linear ordering and zero fill. It is rebuilt on every pressure
projection so active liquid masks, solid faces, and connected components are
always current. Factorization time and storage are emitted in pressure
telemetry and included in tick timings. Enclosed components use a factor-only
deterministic gauge pin at the lowest linear cell, while the physical operator
and zero-mean RHS/Krylov projection remain unchanged. Non-finite or non-positive
pivots return an explicit error with no fallback. Tests cover atmospheric
anchoring, disconnected enclosed single-cell nullspaces, repeated projection
rebuild, and pivot rejection.

### Reuse and allocation investigation

The detailed trace compared each projection with its predecessor by exact active-row set, row order, face sparsity, coefficient bits, component labels, and gauge/atmospheric-anchor signature. Basin had 148/187 unchanged operators (79.1%), canal 95/120 (79.2%), breach 95/120 (79.2%), and scale-2 tunnel 73/124 (58.9%). Geometry stayed fixed; positive-fraction cells only entered the active set in these captures, with no exits. Exact equality is the only candidate for factor reuse. Since the upper-bound factor-time saving for the tunnel is about 89 ms over 120 ticks (roughly 0.74 ms per simulated 1/60 s, or ~2% of all-in cost), while all active rows are changing often and validity checking costs time, exact factor caching is not implemented. RHS, timestep, and warm-start pressure affect solve state, not matrix identity; new geometry, active cells, face connectivity, component partition, atmospheric anchor, or gauge/order changes would invalidate reuse.

An isolated allocation-instrumented capture attributed 561.795 ms / 120 ticks to allocator API calls and recorded 3.472 GB of cumulative allocate bytes (and a matching 3.472 GB deallocated-byte total) for the scale-2 tunnel; the allocator API time was 11.28% of measured tick-step wall time. Peak working sets were 11.78 MiB tunnel and 6.39 MiB basin in these separate traced captures. Reusing two pressure component-mean scratch vectors was also measured; its matched untraced set does not show a reliable timing gain beyond run scatter. ### 30-second basin and conservation correction

The initial basin is filled across its wetted footprint. This separates its stability evidence from the collapsing-block transient. The corrected 30-second run retained 1.479423639 m3 and recorded 0.545576391 m3 cumulative permitted open-top outflow from 2.025000030 m3 initially; global volume error was 1.10e-16 relative (maximum per-tick absolute balance error 9e-15 m3). ### Interface consistency and independent review

Code audit establishes the representation boundary: `project()` sets `liquid = C>0 && !solid`, assembles a full-cell divergence row independent of C, and applies atmospheric pressure at the shared face with any C=0 neighbor (coefficient and face correction doubled for half-cell distance). FCT, by contrast, conservatively transports C using signed donor and limited anti-fluxes based on staggered face velocity. It does not use the pressure row mask as a geometric interface. Projection runs before fraction transport, so a newly wetted cell first receives a pressure row on the following substep; the current velocity is not extended from a reconstructed free-surface field at the time the cell wets.

An independent read-only numerical review reached the same conclusion: C is conserved as mass/volume but does not define subcell interface position, face aperture, pressure sample location, or a shared geometric flux surface. A partial cell gets a full pressure row, and the atmospheric boundary sits at the dry-neighbor face rather than the in-cell interface. The reviewer confirmed that a uniform-depth basin is a continuum hydrostatic equilibrium but is not an equilibrium of this discrete closure. This is a credible structural failure mechanism, not proof that it alone explains secular energy growth.

The single tested representation change was a volume-preserving uniform-column VOF seed, with explicit all-C>0 pressure participation and no threshold. It made the initial geometry physically credible but did not improve settling; the 30-second result is substantially worse than the former uneven seed in surface drift. I did not try a fractional cutoff or an ungrounded row rescaling. A coherent pressure/transport redesign would need one reconstructed geometric interface (e.g. PLIC), shared cut-cell volumes/apertures and atmospheric distances in projection, and geometric swept-volume transport, plus a specified treatment for cut-cell slivers. This is a material numerical redesign. Per the stopping point, obtain a numerical-method review against a reference implementation before another pressure representation change. ## Two-phase candidate: acceptance, energy gate, and cost (2026-09-27)

This section covers the last session's work, which the earlier sections did not record, plus this pass.

### What the two-phase candidate is

`MacGridWorld::set_ambient_density(1.2)` switches the MAC backend to a variable-density two-phase model:

- Every non-solid cell, water or air, gets a pressure row. Face coefficients use the mixture density ρ = ρ_air + C̄(ρ_w − ρ_air).
- Transport uses a Youngs-normal PLIC plane per partial cell. The geometric swept volume is the FCT high-order flux, inside the same conservative shared-face limiter.
- The step order is advection, then gravity on all three components, then pressure, then transport. The legacy order (gravity before advection) let dry-air faces accumulate gravity outside the pressure solve.

The legacy single-phase path is unchanged. It remains the default for bit-for-bit replay of earlier evidence. The debug MAC viewer (`--grid-fluid-demo`) already runs the two-phase model.

### Energy gate correction

The historical `basin` seed is not at rest. It holds 0.600 m of water across 9 × 6 columns, with dry strips inside the same compartment, so it is a small dam break. Leveling to the full-compartment depth of 0.491 m releases about ρgVΔz̄ ≈ 1000·9.81·2.025·0.0545 ≈ 1.08 kJ of potential energy. The two-phase run releases 1.16 kJ on the cell-center PE estimator. KE peaks near 110 J at t ≈ 2.5 s and then decays to about 37–45 J of inviscid sloshing. The last session's corrected Basilisk run reported the same order, about 82 J at 25.5 s. That output was not saved and the WSL build no longer exists, so it is not re-claimed here.

So "≤ 1 J KE gained from rest" is the wrong physical criterion for `basin`. It is the right one for the level `equilibrium` seed. `--acceptance` output is now **version 2**, and the energy gate depends on the scenario:

- `equilibrium`: `kinetic_energy_growth`, meaning KE_end − KE_0 ≤ 1 J. Unchanged.
- `basin`: `mechanical_energy_non_increasing`, meaning max over all ticks of (KE+PE) − (KE_0+PE_0) ≤ 1 J. An inviscid solver may conserve or dissipate energy but never create it.

`kinetic_energy_change_j` is still reported for `basin` as information. No other threshold changed. The KE+PE uses cell-center PE for partial cells, so its per-cell uncertainty is up to about ρg·h³·h/8 ≈ 4.8 J. The remaining discrepancy is that Spall holds about half the sloshing KE. Two MacCormack velocity-advection variants were tried and **removed**:

- **Unrestricted MacCormack** tracked Basilisk's KE for the first 10 s (207/198/146 J vs 230/170/136 J at 3/6/9 s). It then gained about 125 J of total energy between 12 and 24 s, ended at 223 J KE, and failed both late-speed gates (p95 0.675 m/s).
- **MacCormack restricted to single-phase faces** stayed stable but only raised mean KE from 41.5 to 47.9 J, at 2.29 ms.

So the excess dissipation sits at the air/water interface, not in bulk advection. A fix must target interface velocity treatment, for example velocity extrapolation or a consistent momentum/VOF flux. That is recorded as open. The advection code keeps a behavior-preserving `semi_lagrangian` refactor, verified bit-identical over all 1,800 acceptance ticks.

### Multigrid-preconditioned CG

At 13.8k active rows (scale-2 tunnel), MIC(0) needed 72–104 CG iterations per tick, and the count grows with grid size. Each iteration cost about 121 µs for the matrix, 126 µs for the preconditioner and 123 µs for vector work. Two changes:

1. **`PressureOperator` (CSR).** The seven-point operator is assembled once per projection instead of recomputing density-weighted coefficients and index math on every apply. The IC/MIC factor now uses flat CSR rows and an active-indexed in-place back-solve. The reference `apply_pressure_matrix` is kept for tests and diagnostic residual verification. The unit test `assembled_pressure_operator_matches_reference_stencil` checks the two agree. This alone gave about a 5% gain.
2. **`PressurePreconditioner::Multigrid` (`--preconditioner mg`).** One symmetric V-cycle per CG iteration:
   - Galerkin coarse operators A_c = PᵀAP over 2×2×2 piecewise-constant aggregates. These handle the 1000:1 density jump with no rediscretization choices.
   - One forward Gauss–Seidel sweep before and one backward sweep after, with restriction equal to Pᵀ, so the preconditioner is symmetric.
   - Coarsest level (≤ 64 cells) gets 16 symmetric sweep pairs. Precomputed per-level CSR stencils.
   - Enclosed all-Neumann regions stay singular, and CG's existing mean projection handles them.
   - Tests: symmetric to 1e-9 and positive on the tunnel geometry; every level stays diagonally dominant; converges to the same projection as IC(0) (< 1e-6); gameplay invariants hold under both IC(0) and multigrid.
   - One sweep beat two: 39 iterations at 19.8 ms pressure vs 33 at 22.1 ms.

## Wave damping investigation (2026-09-27)

**Question.** The basin comparison showed Spall's late sloshing KE at about half of Basilisk's (42 vs 92 J). Is Spall water over-damped?

**Answer.** Not for resolved waves. The basin gap is extra dissipation during an under-resolved collapse, and every scheme change that removed it made ordinary waves grow. Semi-Lagrangian velocity advection is kept unchanged.

### Standing-wave benchmark (new)

`StandingWaveFixture` in `grid_mac.rs` sets up the textbook first-mode standing wave:

- Tank 6 m long, 3 m tall, 0.5 m wide; closed sides, open top.
- Still depth 1 m, surface η = 0.1 cos(πx/6) m, initialized with exact cell averages.
- Inviscid linear theory gives ω² = gk·tanh(kH), so T = 4.000 s with no amplitude decay.

The end-column antisymmetric elevation is the probe. The runner has `--scenario standing-wave`, and `docs/reports/ENG-103-evidence/reference-standing-wave.c` is its Basilisk twin, with the same initialization and probe, `two-phase.h` and `reduced.h`. The new test `standing_wave_keeps_linear_period_without_numerical_damping_or_growth` runs 20 s and asserts:

- period within 3% of theory;
- amplitude ratio per period in 0.96–1.02;
- no peak above 0.14 m;
- mass conserved to 1e-10 m³.

### Where the basin difference comes from

Using exact-tick sampling (the earlier ±0.25 s rounding in quick tables was wrong), total energy change E − E₀ is:

| Time | Basilisk | Spall |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.5 s | −306 J | −418 J |
| 3 s | −701 J | −727 J |
| 6 s | −949 J | −1,050 J |
| 10 s | −1,029 J | −1,102 J |

Both codes dissipate most of the ~1.1 kJ released within 6 s, while 0.6 m of water collapses into one-cell (0.25 m) dry strips. Spall dissipates about 100 J (~9% of the released energy) more, and that is all missing KE. Because the surviving KE is a small remainder, the gap reads as "2×".

Spall's own basin KE is not converged in this regime: mean KE over 10–30 s is 41.6 J at r = 1, 25.1 J at r = 2, and 32.6 J at dt/2. A 2× Basilisk basin (level 6) was started but advanced only 0.5 s in 10 minutes and was stopped, so no fine-grid reference is claimed.

## Sealed air as isothermal compressible gas (2026-09-27)

**Previous limitation.** In the two-phase solve, air sealed off from the atmosphere (a diving bell, a cave air pocket, a sealed room, or the whole closed-top tunnel) was exactly incompressible. Water could never enter it, whereas real air compresses about 9% at 1 m depth and about 50% at 10 m.

**Model.** Isothermal ideal gas, pV = const, applied per cell to its air fraction: ∇·u = −(1 − C)(p − pⁿ)/(P_abs·dt), with P_abs = 101,325 Pa + pⁿ (floored at 0.1 atm). In this code's pressure units that adds

- diagonal += (1 − C)·ρ_w/(P_abs·dt²)
- RHS += the same factor × pⁿ

The operator stays symmetric positive definite. Any pressure component containing sealed gas is marked anchored, because it is no longer singular: no mean projection and no IC gauge pin. Its pressure level is then physical.

`sealed_air_fractions()` finds sealed air:

- air-dominated cells (C < ½) are flood-filled from the open top and count as vented;
- cells adjacent to vented air, and top-row cells under an open top, share the free surface;
- every other cell with C < 1 contributes its (1 − C).

Water transport is unchanged and still exactly conservative. Water enters a compressing pocket through the ordinary face fluxes. The option is **on by default in the two-phase model** (`set_compressible_enclosed_air`). `--incompressible-air` turns it off, and the runner records `sealed_air` in `grid_physical_model`.

**Diving-bell validation** (new test `compressible_sealed_air_matches_isothermal_diving_bell_equilibrium`):

- Setup: a 3 × 4 × 0.5 m open tank filled to 3.5 m, with an inverted bell (walls and roof, open at the bottom). Its 0.5 × 1 m air column starts dry at 1 atm between 1.5 and 2.5 m.
- Analytic isothermal equilibrium, including the outside level drop, P₀L = (P₀ + ρg·Δh)(L − δ): **δ = 0.150 m**, i.e. 0.0375 m³ inside the bell.
- Measured, mean over 10–20 s: **0.0402 m³ (+7%)**. It settles within about 3 s and holds at 0.0402–0.0403 m³.
- Incompressible control: 8e-12 m³.
- Mass is conserved in both runs. The test asserts a ±25% band and the incompressible control.

**Effect on existing fixtures** (two-phase, multigrid):

- **Canal, breach, closure and scale-1 tunnel:** unchanged to the printed digits (levels, discharge, momenta, roof and pool), at the same cost of 1.27–1.30 ms. Their air is vented or within centimetres of atmospheric.
- **Basin and equilibrium acceptances:** pass (1.14 / 0.74 ms).
- **Standing wave:** unchanged.
- **Closed scale-2 tunnel:** roof and pool are unchanged, and cost falls from 25.3 to **19.0 ms** (p95 37.2 → 27.6 ms), because the sealed region is definite instead of a pinned all-Neumann system.
- **Preconditioner test:** `mic0_and_multigrid_converge_to_the_ic0_projection_on_a_closed_two_phase_tank` now checks both modes. Incompressible air keeps the singular-path divergence assertion; with sealed gas, the divergence in air cells is intended.

**Limits.**

- Isothermal only: no adiabatic heating or cooling, and no gas dissolution.
- Gas mass is not tracked separately. The pocket's volume follows from water conservation and its pressure from the linearized law each step, so pV = const holds to first order per step, not exactly over long horizons.
- The C < ½ connectivity threshold decides whether a thin partial-cell film seals a pocket.
