# ENG-105 integration evidence

Recorded 2026-09-30 on Windows, AMD Ryzen 7 9700X (8 cores / 16 logical CPUs),
Rust workspace toolchain 1.96.1. This completes the bounded integration after
PR #167's partial tick/replica implementation. The remaining coupled increments
are delivered in one follow-up integration PR with separately recorded evidence
and reviewable implementation, recovery and validation commits. The user's 2026-09-29 decision
explicitly permits an exact trapped-volume ledger when sealed placement has
no connected capacity. Body coupling, swimming, boats and appearance are
outside this ticket.

## Authority and ordering

Terrain transactions commit before boundary refresh and displacement, accepted
fluid work, and Rapier. Real `EditIntent` tests excavate a canal, breach a dam,
and fill/reopen the last cell of a fully filled sealed pocket. Fluid fractions
remain separate from terrain occupancy. Excess displaced volume stays attached
to its source cell in a ledger, included in volume/accounting and canonical
persistence; boundary reopening releases it only to face-connected capacity.
The legacy strict prototype boundary API remains available for fixtures that
explicitly require capacity rejection. Authoritative placement uses retention.

A worker receives one immutable grid/exchange clone. The simulation owner
refreshes committed geometry, checks the completed job's boundary revision and
spring rate, and installs it only at a tick boundary. Stale jobs cannot restore
water into an occupied cell. Busy/stale/stability-rejected work reports skipped
fixed fluid time instead of changing server dt. The snapshot supplied to
networking and persistence is exclusively the owner's committed frame.

## Replication and durability

Protocol version 4 / ALPN `spall/4` adds schema-1 tag-18 changed-brick records.
Fractions are quantized to one byte per cell, divided into edge-clipped 16-cell
fluid bricks. Candidates publish atomically only against the installed base
sequence; gaps or invalid data preserve the old presentation. Full compressed
snapshots repair at periodic intervals and after late join. Dense water may
compress better as a full frame; the sender uses the smaller encoding.

Each session/domain has 128 KiB/s application-byte credit and a 128 KiB burst,
with at most 15 Hz updates and a full repair requested every 60 ticks. Budgeted
chunks finish in FIFO order before the next frame is chosen. A bounded reliable
backlog disconnects an overflowing recipient for re-baselining. The byte count
includes encoded records plus a 32-byte envelope allowance, excluding QUIC
retransmission and IP headers. With eight regions, the per-client ceiling is
1 MiB/s plus 1 MiB burst. Viewer publications contain every installed domain;
world reset clears both assemblers and render input.

Save schema 2 publishes exact canonical water groups and checksum rows in the
same checkpoint transaction/cursor as terrain and bodies. Ordered journal
batches contain topology/poses followed by the committed canonical water group.
Exact IEEE fraction/ledger bits, config, source/gate state, fluid time and
cumulative source/drain/open-outflow volumes survive recovery. Velocities,
pressure, sealed-air pressure/cache and sleep reset to rest/awake. This preserves
water amounts rather than promising identical post-restart trajectories.

Recovery requires explicit rows even for empty groups and rejects missing or
corrupt canonical data. Old schema-1 saves must be copied using:

```powershell
cargo xtask migrate-water-save --source old/world.db --output migrated/world.db
```

The source is unchanged, existing destinations are refused and the migrated
save is recovered for verification. Old saves cannot recover water they never
stored; the new water group starts empty. Older binaries reject schema 2.

## Dormancy and residency envelope

Up to eight independently authored, non-overlapping pressure domains share a
4,194,304 fluid-cell ceiling. Each voxel domain is bounded to 32 million cells;
coarsening is 1–8. Checkpoint water bytes are bounded to 128 MiB and each source
list to 65,536 coordinates. Canonical group validation precedes solver allocation.
These are rejection limits, not promises of real-time performance at the ceiling.

A region sleeps after 60 accepted quiet steps with maximum face component speed
at most 1e-5 m/s and no active springs/drains. Terrain commits and restored
residency wake it; enabled gated springs keep it active. Flowing neighbors inside
a pressure domain participate in the same solve. Unknown terrain bricks pause
the entire region without changing fractions or guessing air, drains or walls;
resumption captures current committed geometry and invalidates stale work.

The declared side boundaries are closed; an authored open top accounts outflow.
Connected waterways must fit one pressure domain. Independent domains do not
exchange water across their sides. Seamless pressure/exchange across streaming
grids and spatial water interest filtering remain scaling extensions; this
integration conservatively suspends an authored domain on residency loss.

## Measurements and checks

- Three complete scale-1 ENG-103 reservoirs, each 24 x 12 x 8 cells at 0.25 m,
  6,912 fluid cells total, fixed 1/60 s outer dt, eight maximum substeps, 600 ticks.
  Release total mean **3.423 ms/tick**, fluid mean **3.399 ms/tick**, total p95
  **4.164 ms**, p99 **4.645 ms**, peak **4.974 ms**. Initial volumes were
  `[2.3000000342726707; 3]` m³; every region stayed within 1e-8 m³ throughout.
  Restored canonical group bits matched the saved group. A repeat with explicit
  assertions that every one of the three solvers accepts a step on every tick,
  while the workspace suite ran concurrently, measured total mean 5.356 ms,
  fluid mean 5.326 ms, p95 7.377 ms, p99 8.477 ms and peak 9.007 ms. The
  workload was identical; competing CPU activity affects measured timing.
  A prior debug run
  averaged 36.234 ms/tick; debug timing does not establish a 60 Hz gate.
- Three-region live QUIC run: two clients, the second joining during flow,
  real dam action, then world reset. Both installed all three domains without
  water errors and matched final terrain hashes. **193,825 application bytes**
  queued across the run, **564 full frames**, zero delta frames (full frames
  compressed smaller). Server bounded to 420 ticks; this debug test is
  correctness/bandwidth evidence, not a paced real-time performance acceptance.
- Sparse-update budget test: 131,072 incompressible cells, 600 simulated ticks,
  one changed cell. **1,314,506 bytes** queued over 10 simulated seconds;
  exactly one delta record and final presentation bytes matched. At every tick,
  bytes stayed below initial burst plus rate times elapsed simulation time.
- Real sealed-pocket placement/reopening retained and released the entire
  0.015625 m³ cell volume, with error below 1e-12 m³ on every tick.
- Save/restart recovered exact canonical fractions and retained volume at rest.
  An interrupted journal commit recovered only the durable preceding water
  state and matching geometry. Missing/corrupt water rows/table were rejected;
  copy migration preserved the source bytes and historical geometry.

## Limits and next work

The earlier 2 ms proposed water allocation is **not met** by the aggregate
three-reservoir run. Its measured total p95/p99 fit the 12/16.7 ms server targets
for this bounded fixture only; this is not whole-game/G4 acceptance. Larger
ENG-103 pressure-cost failures remain, worker clones retain additional grid
memory, and journaling exact groups each tick incurs size-dependent disk cost.
Hardware presentation captures, retransmission bandwidth and long production
soaks were not run. Dynamic bodies remain ignored as fluid obstacles.

Next existing unblocked task: **ENG-104**, hands-on confirmation of the local
water demo. Future body coupling and cross-domain scaling need separate scoped
tickets; no new T00–T25 assignment is invented by this integration.
