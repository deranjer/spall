# ENG-120: fluid domain growth near digging

User report: after hammering out ground beside a lake, water flowed and then
stopped at an invisible boundary. Cause: an authored fluid domain is the water's
bounding box plus a 1 m horizontal / 1 m above / 0.5 m below margin, with closed
sides (ENG-105). Terrain outside it is not simulated, so a canal dug past the box
never receives water. This ticket lets a domain grow, server-side and
authoritatively.

## Behaviour

- Opt-in: `WaterSetup::growth: Option<WaterGrowth>` (default `None`, so fixtures
  and existing worlds are unchanged). The worldgen scene enables the default
  policy: trigger 16 voxels, margin 32 voxels, cap 3,000,000 voxel cells.
- After each tick's committed terrain edits, every terrain-editing op of a
  transaction is a separate voxel-cell box (`transaction_world_boxes`; the old
  union box let a collapse far away stretch one box across the arena). A box
  that is not wholly inside the domain but within `trigger_voxels` of it grows
  the domain, per axis it extends past, to cover the box plus `margin_voxels`.
  The result stays on the old fluid-cell grid (coarsening 1-8), is clipped to the
  world's brick bounds, and is rejected when it exceeds `max_voxel_cells`, 32 M
  voxels, the 4 M fluid-cell protocol limit, overlaps another region, or covers a
  brick that is not resident (growing there would pause the whole region). A
  too-large or overlapping plan is retried with margin 0 before being refused.
  Refusals are counted (`WaterTickMetrics::growth_refused`), never truncated.
- `AuthoritativeWater::grown` builds the replacement from the owner's committed
  grid: exact fractions, trapped ledger, cumulative outflow, spring/drain
  accounting, sources/sinks, gated rate, execution mode (inline or worker) and
  the frame sequence all carry over, so volume is conserved exactly. New cells
  start dry. **Velocity and pressure restart at rest**, as after recovery; a flow
  in progress loses its momentum at the growth tick. The old worker thread is
  joined when the old region is dropped.
- Persistence: canonical water state already stores origin and dimensions, so
  saves recover the grown domain. Growth policy is scene configuration, not
  saved state: `serve` reapplies it to recovered regions
  (`Simulation::set_water_growth`). Recovered regions still run inline; that is
  pre-existing (`AuthoritativeWater::restore` always builds an inline engine).
- Replication: a region's identity on the wire is its origin. A grown region may
  move its origin (growth toward -x/-y/-z). The server drops publisher state for
  origins that no longer exist; the client drops any region whose box a newly
  assembled keyframe overlaps with a different origin
  (`WaterKeyframe::overlaps`), so the old extent is not drawn alongside the new.
  Same-origin growth sends a full keyframe (a delta needs equal dimensions).
  Wire format unchanged.

## Evidence

Tests (all pass): `crates/spall_sim/tests/water_growth.rs` (8): an edit wholly
inside never grows; a fixed domain has no cells past its box and water stays in;
growth conserves volume to 1e-9 m^3 across the growth tick and water then flows
into a tunnel past the old box; a worker region stays on its worker and keeps
publishing; over-cap refusal is counted and changes nothing; margin shrinks to
avoid a neighbour and growth is refused when even the bare edit overlaps one;
growth waits for non-resident bricks; a grown domain round-trips through
canonical state. Plus `WaterKeyframe::overlaps` and the client's superseded-region
replacement.

Measured on this machine (Windows, AMD Ryzen 7 9700X), release build, seed-1
512-cell showcase world, water on its worker: initial domain 118 x 46 x 128 voxels
(694,784; 86,848 fluid cells at coarsening 2). 44 hammer-sized cuts (radius 3
voxels, one every 6 ticks) dug +x from the lake's east shore for 40 voxels past
the old edge, then 3,600 paced ticks.

| | growth on | growth off (control) |
|---|---|---|
| growths | 3 (domains 0.89 M, 1.10 M, 1.30 M voxels) | 0 |
| tick that grew | 52.5, 65.3, 90.3 ms | - |
| worst tick | 90.3 ms | 35.8 ms |
| water volume change over the run | 4.4e-12 m^3 of 642.48 | -7.1e-12 |
| water past the old domain edge | 39.2-39.5 m^3 | 0.000 m^3 |
| completed solver steps (median / p95 / max) | 101.7 / 138.2 / 190.0 ms | 21.8 / 27.8 / 75.0 ms |
| skipped fluid steps | 749 (37.5 s) | 102 (5.1 s) |

The control reproduces the reported bug (no water beyond the box). The solver
step is over the 1/60 s budget in both runs: 21.8 ms even without growth, and
about 5x that while water is actually flowing in the larger domain, so water
moves slower than real time (fluid time is skipped, not the server tick). That
cost is the existing water-solver budget (ENG-103/105), not growth overhead; this
ticket does not close it.

## Limits and follow-ups

- Growth tick cost scales with the new domain (~50-70 ns per voxel cell for the
  boundary capture, grid build and restore) and runs on the simulation thread:
  expect a 50-90 ms hitch at 1 M voxels, about 0.2 s at the 3 M cap. Every edit
  near a grown domain also re-captures the whole boundary, so edits near large
  domains get slower. Building the grown grid on the water worker and installing
  at a tick boundary, and an incremental boundary capture, are the follow-ups.
- A domain is one box. Digging sideways grows the whole cross-section.
- Overlapping regions are not merged (needs a joint pressure domain).
- Vegetation growth edits near a lake also count as edits and can grow the
  domain; the cap bounds it.
- Not run: the interactive, GPU-rendered hands-on check of the grown water look.
