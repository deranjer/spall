# ENG-121: water solver cost

Goal: make generated-world water run at real time, accepting some accuracy.
Evidence is from this machine (Windows, AMD Ryzen 7 9700X), release builds, the
seed-1 `showcase` world, water on its worker at 20 Hz (50 ms per fluid step).
The scenario digs 44-46 hammer-sized cuts (radius 3 voxels) east from the lake
shore for 40 voxels past the old domain edge, then runs 3,000 paced ticks.

## What the time was spent on

Per fluid step, 512-cell arena at 0.5 m cells (87,000 fluid cells; ~6,000 wet),
water flowing into the trench: pressure projection 63-73%, FCT transport ~26%,
velocity advection ~8%. Inside the projection the multigrid preconditioner is
~60% (about 0.6 ms per CG iteration per 23k rows), with ~28 iterations per solve.
Cost scales with wet cells x iterations, not domain size, and pressure grows to
~90% on the larger arena.

The 1024-cell default arena was far worse: 347,000 fluid cells, median 700-785 ms
per 50 ms step, i.e. water at **0.05-0.06x real time** (effectively frozen).

## Changes

1. **Adaptive coarsening for generated worlds** (`worldgen_scene`): the finest
   coarsening 2..=8 whose domain fits 50,000 fluid cells. 512 arena: 3 (0.75 m);
   1024 arena: 4 (1 m). Hand-authored scenes keep coarsening 2.
2. **FCT transport touches only cells with a non-trivial face.** The donor
   limiter (up to 64 passes per substep) used to sweep and re-allocate four
   domain-sized vectors every pass. Results are bit-identical (same fraction
   checksum before and after); transport 10.9 -> 6.9 ms at 0.5 m cells.
3. **Brick-wise `SolidBoundary::capture`** (one snapshot per brick, not one hash
   lookup per cell): ~4x faster. It runs on the simulation thread for every
   edit near water and when a domain grows. A test compares it to the per-cell
   reference, including the non-resident-brick error.
4. **Growth no longer repeats its boundary capture** in the tick that grew, and
   when a cap is the limit it refuses instead of regrowing by a sliver per swing
   (measured: ten growths of 400-470 ms in a row on the 1024 arena before).
   The growth cap is now twice the fluid-cell budget.

## Accuracy cost

- A coarse cell is solid when at least half of its voxels are (existing rule).
  At 0.75 m a one-voxel wall (9 of 27) no longer blocks water; at 1 m a wall
  under two voxels does not. Natural terrain and thick built walls are fine;
  thin player-built dams leak.
- Shoreline water lost to cells that coarsen to solid: 4.7% of the seeded volume
  at 0.5 m, 5.6% at 0.75 m (reported in `WaterSeedReport`, not hidden).
- A 1.5 m wide trench is 2 cells wide at 0.75 m; flow there is more diffusive.

## Tried and rejected

- **Looser pressure tolerance** (1e-8 -> 1e-4): pressure drops ~35% (27 -> 11
  iterations) but the donor limiter then needs many more passes, so total time is
  flat (before the limiter change it was 70% worse). Left at default.
- **Higher CFL ceiling:** no effect; steps already use one substep.
- **Shorter/longer fluid dt:** cost per simulated second is roughly constant
  (0.29x at 1/60, 0.34x at 1/20, 0.37x at 1/30, 0.34x at 1/15 at 0.5 m).
- **Sleeping an idle lake.** A resting generated lake never sleeps: its internal
  currents stay at 1-3 m/s (parasitic currents over staircase terrain) and the
  surface keeps moving ~0.9 cell-fractions per second after the initial transient
  (kinetic energy 5.8 kJ after one step, 42 kJ at 4 steps). Velocity drag of
  0.5-2 per second did not settle it (the flow is driven, not a decaying slosh).
  Sleeping on a still surface (< 2 fractions/s) stalls flow into a trench
  (12.6 m^3 instead of 58.9 m^3), so it is unsafe. Not shipped.

## Remaining

- An idle lake still costs about 15 ms of every 50 ms step (30% of a worker
  thread) at 0.75 m cells. The cause is the unbalanced rest state above; a
  well-balanced free-surface/terrain discretization or a settled initial state
  would let it sleep.
- Pressure (multigrid-preconditioned CG, ~28 iterations) is O(wet cells x
  iterations); a better preconditioner or active-region restriction is the next
  lever if finer water is wanted on large worlds.
- Not run: interactive hands-on check of the coarser water in the game window.

## Rest-state follow-up (2026-10-02)

Main includes this stack through PR182. A bounded diagnostic now distinguishes
resolved submerged stairs (stable) from omitted subcell solids seeded as
water/air mixtures (persistent currents despite converged pressure). Pressure
rows in the two-phase path include non-solid air cells as well as water cells;
the earlier wet-cell scaling description is incomplete. See
[rest-state investigation](ENG-121-rest-state.md) for measured evidence,
integration checks and the next assignment, ENG-122. No production solver
tolerance, sleep rule or fluid representation changed in this investigation.
