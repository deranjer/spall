## Increment 12: conservative staggered momentum coupling (2026-10-03)

`PhasePressureWorld::enable_conservative_momentum` opts into first-order
staggered mixture momentum advection. It skips semi-Lagrangian velocity
sampling, applies forces and fine pressure, advances strict PLIC water, then
consumes **the final accepted donor + antidiffusive + path-repaired transfers**.
The gas-volume flux is full projected face-volume flux minus accepted liquid
flux. The mixture mass flux is `rho_air * F_volume + (rho_water-rho_air) * F_water`.
Each primal flux contributes two half-cell lanes to every component's velocity
control volumes. Identical dual edges are combined before selecting the donor.
No second water limiter, reconstruction, amount clipping or water cutoff.

Momentum and mass are transferred together by upwinding on these dual edges.
Each control volume uses half the mixture mass of its incident open cells,
matching the existing pressure face-density stencil. First-order upwinding
dissipates kinetic energy; this is not high-order interface accuracy acceptance.
Wall-normal momentum delivered to blocked half-volumes is booked as wall
reaction, and atmospheric boundary momentum has a signed ledger. Atmospheric
gas inflow has prescribed zero velocity. These are **advection-only mixture
ledgers**, excluding gravity and pressure impulses, and do not assert separate
liquid momentum conservation through phase mixing or body coupling.

To keep upwinding convex during liquid/gas throughflow, momentum alone may
subcycle over the same accepted physical interval and fixed final mass fluxes.
The minimum old/final dual mass bounds donor withdrawal with a 0.9 safety
factor, at most 1,024 subcycles. Negative/nonfinite mass, budget exhaustion or a
dual-mass mismatch greater than `1e-8 * max(old_mass,new_phase_mass)` rejects
the candidate. This mass guard is unchanged across all attempts. The adapter's
immutable clone validates transport, both pressure projections, final phase
fractions/basins and retained memory before installing any state.

A second fine pressure projection restores incompressibility after momentum
and density move. Its pressure is an incremental impulse; the stored pressure
is force pressure plus this correction, preserving lake-head diagnostics and
the next warm start. Both projections must converge. Opt-in relative tolerance
is `min(caller_tolerance,1e-12)`; absolute tolerance is
`min(caller_tolerance,1e-8)`, retaining the original absolute accuracy contract.
The temporary 1e-12 absolute setting stalled a closed incompressible rest solve
at a 5.39e-10 residual. Read-only verification established this was separate
from the sealed-gas mass rejection; restoring the original absolute tolerance
passes that comparison while **still requiring the sealed-gas test to reject**.
Automatic review initially rejected the tolerance restoration, then allowed it
after this evidence. No failed mass gate was bypassed or tolerance relaxed
below the original reference contract. Default velocity advection and pressure
configuration remain unchanged.

## Increment 11: balanced phase-aware pressure preconditioning

`PhasePressureWorld::enable_pressure_preconditioner` opts into
`B = Q + (I-QA) S (I-AQ)`, with `Q = P B_c P^T`, the physical fine operator
`A`, the existing fine preconditioner `S`, and eight symmetric coarse
Gauss-Seidel sweeps `B_c`. This symmetric combination is suitable for CG:
`x^T B x = x^T Q x + y^T S y`, with `y = (I-AQ)x`. The owning solver retains
its existing component-mean projection and fine convergence contract. No new
gauge pins, changed stopping tolerance, or final coarse pressure unknowns.
The one-shot predictor remains available separately; balanced mode does not
also apply it at startup. The default MAC path retains its prior arithmetic.

The physical sparse row matrix is assembled once per projection and its CSR
smoother is cached for that solve. Each preconditioner application reuses three
row vectors and two dense fine vectors without allocation. It requires two
additional fine operator applications. Exact geometry and wet-edge refresh
rules remain unchanged, including conservative rebuilds when an interface
predicate changes. Memory reporting counts this additional transient numerical
payload, not whole-process peak/RSS, candidate clones or all solver scratch.
The retained-array cap is still a retained cap; it does not bound these

## Increment 9: phase-aware pressure basis and shared transfer routing

`phase_graph::PhaseGraph` builds a snapshot-local piecewise-constant pressure
basis. Within each exact coarse open component it groups wet fine cells only
through actual liquid overlap, and groups dry cells separately. Two pools
connected through air remain separate wet rows. Tiny positive fractions are
retained. A partially filled fine cell is still one mixture-pressure unknown,
as in the fine MAC reference; this is not an independent air/liquid pressure
model. Every open fine cell belongs to exactly one row. Connections aggregate
real fine faces by row pair and axis, including faces inside a coarse cell.

The pressure operator accepts coefficients supplied by a physical solver and
computes the Galerkin restriction `P^T A P`. Tests compare it with direct fine
face application, including heterogeneous weights, top boundary coefficients,
symmetry, energy and fine-face counts. This algebraic equality holds for the

existing standalone phase transport and routes those same transfers into row
water and three-axis liquid momentum ledgers using caller-supplied donor
velocities. A blocked transfer therefore moves neither mass nor momentum.

not velocity advection, staggered momentum installation, wall-force accounting

remain immutable on failure, including late nonfinite momentum rejection.

Graphs bind to the exact immutable geometry/fraction snapshot; changed fine

## Increment 8: strict paired-flux channel repair

The previous limiter scaled all faces from stale cell sums (Jacobi iteration).
In saturated paths this moved a bound violation only one edge per pass; loops
could keep circulating it. The strict reference now applies Gauss-Seidel face
corrections, immediately updates both endpoint sums, and alternates traversal
direction. Only a violating donor or receiver reduces a shared transfer. An
actual reduction rounds inward with `next_down`; amounts are never clipped.
Balanced saturated cycles remain untouched and useful throughflow is retained.

Local sweeps still have the original 64-pass ceiling. If those cannot resolve
the graph, a bounded global repair searches along existing directed transfers
for actual available capacity. Overflow returns water along incoming paths;
underflow refunds outgoing transfers along forward paths. The same reduction
is applied to every path edge, including accounted open-boundary transfers,
so intermediate saturated cells can pass the correction through. Fresh face
sums validate the entire candidate after each repair. Searches traverse only
existing fine fluid faces: no invented wall crossing or guessed water placement.

The additional repair budget is at most 64 path augmentations (and at most
touched-cell count plus edge count). Exhaustion, a nonfinite candidate, no
reachable capacity or no representable change rejects the candidate; the
existing `PhasePressureWorld` clone still makes late rejection atomic. This
is a different connected-path algorithm, not extra local limiter iterations
or a relaxed fraction bound. It does not prove all possible graphs acceptable.

CSR adjacency, BFS parents and queue are allocated only on this fallback and
indexed by the sorted touched-cell set, rather than every domain cell. Cursors
are reused as parents. Telemetry reports additional scratch array capacities
(offsets, incident-edge indices, parents, queue), excluding existing FCT scratch,
pressure/clone/phase validation memory and allocator overhead. The retained
phase/solver arrays are unchanged. The 1024 production peak-memory gate remains
unmeasured and unaccepted.

## Increment 3: hydrostatic reference and paired capacity limiter

The pressure operator now builds a horizontal hydrostatic reference separately
for each connected open region. It sums the region's water and inverts integer
open-layer capacities to obtain a reference level, then integrates two-phase
hydrostatic pressure at component centroids. Solid capacity never enters air
fraction. Density departures from that reference remain buoyancy forces;
horizontal gravity and predicted volume flux remain active. The projection
solves for perturbation pressure and retains the explicit reference pressure.
This changes the experimental gravity discretization; it is not equivalent to
the previous volume-averaged gravity operator on arbitrary moving interfaces.

At equilibrium, equal actual/reference densities cancel before multiplication
and before CG, rather than adding and numerically solving away large gravity
and pressure terms. Subtract each node's actual and reference density **before**
summing their departures. The initial sum-then-subtract implementation passed
one second of rest but grew roundoff into 1.21 m/s artificial currents over a
minute in the 1024 world. That failed intermediate result is retained in
`ENG-122-reference-arithmetic-failure.jsonl`. The final per-node subtraction
passes the full minute; no velocity threshold, sleep, damping or skipped
operator iteration is involved.

Transport now computes all donor-water transfers, sums simultaneous incoming
and outgoing amounts, and scales each violating paired transfer identically at
donor and receiver. A receiver can use capacity made available by simultaneous
outflow. Full chains and cycles therefore continue to transport, while water
cannot exceed open capacity. A tiny roundoff safety factor applies only to
transfer scales that already violate a bound; component amounts are never
clamped. The limiter is bounded to 64 reductions and fails atomically if the
bounds remain unresolved. Metrics report passes, moved and limited water.
Volume flux/velocity is not overwritten by a still-water heuristic.

## Increment 1: geometry and seeding

## Design constraint and implemented representation

A single capacity scalar cannot represent a thin wall inside a coarse cell:
the open spaces on opposite sides become one pressure/transport unknown. Face
aperture averages alone also invent a connection when openings on adjacent
cells do not line up. Both are forbidden shortcuts for player-built dams.

`spall_fluid::cut_cell::CutCellGeometry` consumes an immutable, fully resident
`SolidBoundary` snapshot. It preserves every six-connected open component
inside each coarse cell (factor 1 through 8), each component's integer voxel
capacity, centroid and vertical layer counts. Matched open fine faces connect
components across coarse-cell boundaries; diagonal contact and misaligned
openings do not connect. Domain sides have no portal. No new physics engine,
ECS entities, global state or runtime dependency is introduced.

Component and portal ordering is deterministic. Array indices belong only to
this snapshot and must not be persisted as stable identities. Geometry anchors
are fine global coordinates, not ECS or library IDs. Explicit fine-cell,
component and portal limits fail rather than returning reduced topology. Worst
case component count approaches the fine-grid open count; coarsening alone does
not guarantee a performance bound. Build queues visit at most one coarse cell.

`aggregate_amounts` validates fine-domain water fractions and sums into each
component's actual capacity. Water overlapping solid, invalid/out-of-domain
amount vectors and non-finite values fail. No seed volume is discarded into a
majority-solid coarse cell, filled into omitted solid space, or counted as air.
Layer counts give exact open volume below a horizontal surface; they are not
a general advection/free-surface model.
