## Increment 7: fine phase/pressure reference; channel gate fails

`phase_pressure::PhasePressureWorld` couples the retained fine phase fractions
to the existing two-phase MAC/PLIC solver at the actual 0.25 m voxel size.
Every fine open voxel gets its own pressure degree of freedom, including pools
inside one coarse component connected only through air. This is a deliberately
named **fine-grid reference**, not the compressed component solver. The packed
phase topology is shared through `Arc<[u32]>` between immutable candidates.
Canonical fine fractions remain f64; component totals never reconstruct them.

Each outer step clones the numerical solver candidate, requires convergence in
every pressure substep, validates exact phase bounds and basin limits, and only
then installs both fraction and solver state. Pressure, transport, fragmentation
and retained-array-limit failures leave the original state untouched. The
constructor rejects scale mismatch and checks dense initial array requirements
before creating the MAC copy. The retained array cap includes phase and MAC
arrays, but excludes shared geometry, allocator overhead, transient pressure,
validation and candidate-clone allocations. It is not a peak-memory/RSS budget.

MAC's historical FCT accepts excursions within 1e-10; the phase representation
requires fractions strictly in [0,1]. The reference therefore opts into a final
64-pass paired limiter of accepted donor-plus-PLIC face transfers. It reuses
FCT scratch, reduces shared transfers rather than clipping amounts, and
recomputes open-boundary and tracked-region ledgers from the final transfers.
Default callers retain the historical arithmetic. Net incoming-minus-outgoing
is formed before adding it to the original fraction so exactly balanced full
cycles retain their values. Tests preserve those cycles, signed open-boundary
accounting and bounded rejection of an unresolved saturated chain.

## Increment 6: packed faces and two-array transport scratch

The phase snapshot now stores each fine face in one `u32`: thirty bits of
domain-local lower index and two axis bits. The upper endpoint is derived from
the immutable domain stride. Endpoints, face ordering, geometry and f64 water
fractions are unchanged. The constructor rejects domains beyond the packed
index ceiling (2^30 fine cells) before building faces, rather than wrapping.
This ceiling is far above the probes' existing four-million-cell allocation
limit. `faces()` returns an allocation-free decoded view whose iterator yields
the original `PhaseFace` values; current callers keep the same ordered flux
mapping. Packed indices are derived snapshot data, not canonical or wire IDs.

Transport retains one transfer array and two fine-cell arrays. Its CFL outgoing
array becomes the limiter's outgoing sum; incoming/outgoing sums become receiver/
donor scales in place on rejected candidates. On success, incoming becomes the
candidate amount array. Arithmetic order and paired scaling are preserved.
The outgoing and transfer arrays are dropped before basin validation. There
are no separate full candidate, donor-scale or receiver-scale arrays, and no
fresh cell arrays per limiter pass. Numeric scratch telemetry reports allocated

## Increment 5: retain water placement and derive wet basins/faces

The experimental `phase_water` module retains horizontal water fractions at
fine-voxel locations in the immutable exact cut-cell geometry. This is a bounded
CPU reference representation, not per-voxel entities and not an activated fluid
solver. Equal coarse component totals can describe water on opposite sides of
a low wall or near/away from a portal; they cannot determine hydraulic state.
The fine reference retains that information explicitly instead of inventing a
pool allocation after aggregation.

Water basin connectivity uses actual positive face overlap, with no small-water
threshold. Adjacent horizontal faces overlap by the smaller horizontal fill;
vertical faces connect water only when the lower voxel reaches its top and the
upper contains water at its bottom. Air connections and dry vertical gaps do
not join pools. Basin anchors are deterministic snapshot labels, not persistent
identities. Portal summaries expose existing overlap and separate directional
donor areas: zero overlap with a dry receiver must not prevent real inflow.
Fine faces within a coarse component are retained as well as coarse portals.

A standalone prescribed-fine-flux transport operator uses those donor areas,
simultaneous paired capacity limits and a bounded 64-pass failure path. It
conserves water without clipping amounts, permits saturated throughflow and
limits downward transfer to available water. Invalid input, CFL excess and
basin-count overflow reject atomically, including fragmentation after transport.
Domain/face/basin allocation limits are explicit. This operator has no pressure,
momentum, source/sink or open-top step and does not guess a fine flux field from
coarse portal totals. Uniform face velocities and horizontal fine interfaces
are first-order assumptions; general interface orientation and swept interface
motion still need acceptance. In particular a partial lower voxel has no wet
upward face until water reaches its top, which is not general PLIC transport.

Twelve phase tests pass, covering:

- Opposite pools inside one air-connected coarse component retain different
  phase placement despite identical component amounts.
- Pools below a crest remain separate; overtopped pools join.
- Partial horizontal overlap, dry vertical gaps and tiny retained water.
- Equal coarse totals with different directional portal apertures.
- Donor flow into dry receivers, a real one-voxel opening, saturated chains,
  bounded downward transfer and amount conservation.
- Six seconds of prescribed divergence-free air circulation above the original
  low-dam crest carry zero water and preserve the fine phase snapshot exactly.
- Invalid seeds/scales, allocation limits, CFL and fragmentation rejection.

The prescribed-air test establishes phase transport behavior, **not** acceptance
of the coupled low-dam gate. The unchanged component pressure/momentum solver
was rerun with its original `low-dam 600` fixture and still fails (exit 1),
with 0.000272616 m3 downstream. Its single pressure/amount row per open component
and hydrostatic reference per air-connected region have not been replaced.
The next unblocked ENG-122 item is phase/basin-aware pressure and momentum
coupling, including internal basin degrees of freedom, then rerun the unchanged
low-dam, channel and generated-world dynamic gates. Wet portal summaries alone
cannot repair an internally merged pool.

## Increment 4: momentum advection and a failed low-dam gate

The experimental operator now advects staggered mixture momentum using the same
limited water transfers as amount transport. Aperture weights partition each
component's water/air mass among normal velocity faces and zero-normal wall
faces. First-order donor scatter records wall impulse and open-top momentum
export/import. The pressure mobility uses this same dual mass, making projection
nonincreasing in its staggered kinetic-energy norm for closed geometry. These
weights are an approximation for irregular geometry, not a general accuracy gate.

The coupled operation performs force projection, shared amount/momentum
transport, then projection without another gravity impulse. Every stage operates
on a candidate; even failure of the final projection leaves original amounts,
fluxes and ledgers unchanged. Momentum balance metrics cover the advection
stage only; they do not validate the complete pressure/gravity wall-force budget.
Potential energy uses bottom-filled horizontal reconstruction per component and
is a diagnostic approximation, not PLIC interface reconstruction.

## Increment 3: hydrostatic reference and paired capacity limiter

The pressure operator now builds a horizontal hydrostatic reference separately
for each connected open region. It sums the region's water and inverts integer
open-layer capacities to obtain a reference level, then integrates two-phase
hydrostatic pressure at component centroids. Solid capacity never enters air
fraction. Density departures from that reference remain buoyancy forces;
horizontal gravity and predicted volume flux remain active. The projection
solves for perturbation pressure and retains the explicit reference pressure.
This changes the experimental gravity discretization; it is not equivalent to
the previous volume-averaged gravity operator on arbitrary moving interfaces.

At equilibrium, equal actual/reference densities cancel before multiplication
and before CG, rather than adding and numerically solving away large gravity
and pressure terms. Subtract each node's actual and reference density **before**
summing their departures. The initial sum-then-subtract implementation passed
one second of rest but grew roundoff into 1.21 m/s artificial currents over a
minute in the 1024 world. That failed intermediate result is retained in
`ENG-122-reference-arithmetic-failure.jsonl`. The final per-node subtraction
passes the full minute; no velocity threshold, sleep, damping or skipped
operator iteration is involved.

Transport now computes all donor-water transfers, sums simultaneous incoming
and outgoing amounts, and scales each violating paired transfer identically at
donor and receiver. A receiver can use capacity made available by simultaneous
outflow. Full chains and cycles therefore continue to transport, while water
cannot exceed open capacity. A tiny roundoff safety factor applies only to
transfer scales that already violate a bound; component amounts are never
clamped. The limiter is bounded to 64 reductions and fails atomically if the
bounds remain unresolved. Metrics report passes, moved and limited water.
Volume flux/velocity is not overwritten by a still-water heuristic.

Fourteen operator tests pass, including:

- Analytic hydrostatic water/air pressure and the earlier analytic pressure drop.
- One minute of partial flat-surface rest across irregular submerged geometry,
  with both 0.5 and non-binary 0.3 fine-cell surface fractions.
- Thirty seconds of unequal-level flow: the higher side transfers water to the
  lower side and total water plus outflow remains within 1e-12 m3.
- An existing divergence-free full-cycle flux survives projection and moves
  water without limiter reductions; a saturated chain also retains throughflow.
- Near-full receivers and completely full receivers limit paired transfers
  conservatively. A blocked 100-node chain exhausts the limiter and leaves all
  state unchanged, exercising the bounded failure path.
- Existing thin-wall isolation, known donor transfer and rejected CFL/pressure
  state preservation.

## Increment 1: geometry and seeding

## Design constraint and implemented representation

A single capacity scalar cannot represent a thin wall inside a coarse cell:
the open spaces on opposite sides become one pressure/transport unknown. Face
aperture averages alone also invent a connection when openings on adjacent
cells do not line up. Both are forbidden shortcuts for player-built dams.

`spall_fluid::cut_cell::CutCellGeometry` consumes an immutable, fully resident
`SolidBoundary` snapshot. It preserves every six-connected open component
inside each coarse cell (factor 1 through 8), each component's integer voxel
capacity, centroid and vertical layer counts. Matched open fine faces connect
components across coarse-cell boundaries; diagonal contact and misaligned
openings do not connect. Domain sides have no portal. No new physics engine,
ECS entities, global state or runtime dependency is introduced.

Component and portal ordering is deterministic. Array indices belong only to
this snapshot and must not be persisted as stable identities. Geometry anchors
are fine global coordinates, not ECS or library IDs. Explicit fine-cell,
component and portal limits fail rather than returning reduced topology. Worst
case component count approaches the fine-grid open count; coarsening alone does
not guarantee a performance bound. Build queues visit at most one coarse cell.

`aggregate_amounts` validates fine-domain water fractions and sums into each
component's actual capacity. Water overlapping solid, invalid/out-of-domain
amount vectors and non-finite values fail. No seed volume is discarded into a
majority-solid coarse cell, filled into omitted solid space, or counted as air.
Layer counts give exact open volume below a horizontal surface; they are not
a general advection/free-surface model.

## Geometry acceptance tests

Seven tests in `crates/spall_fluid/tests/cut_cell_geometry.rs` cover:

- One-voxel walls inside coarse cells at factors 3-8 keep separate spaces even
  though the old majority mask loses the wall.
- A one-voxel opening reconnects the spaces.
- Apertures require matching fine voxels on both sides; misaligned openings
  remain disconnected, while a matching opening has exactly one fine face.
- The unaligned stair fixture from the previous investigation retains all
  138.375 m3 of seed water. Saturated open components contain water rather than
  fictitious air, including majority-solid coarse cells.
- Partial horizontal surfaces integrate correct solid-excluding capacity.
- For all 256 patterns of eight occupancy decisions extruded along Z, component
  graph reachability matches independent fine-grid flood fill. Domains cross
  negative brick coordinates. Repeated snapshots compare equal.
- Invalid factors, dimensions, amounts and capacity/build limits return errors.

These verify topology and amounts, not momentum, pressure equilibrium,
displacement after edits, sealed-air behavior or durability.

## Integration decision and remaining implementation

**Keep the snapshot experimental; do not activate it in the production solver
yet.** Geometry/capacity tests pass, but an aperture/capacity-only modification
to the existing one-row-per-coarse-cell solver would reconnect the isolated
spaces that these tests prove exist. The next increment needs a component-aware
finite-volume projection and conservative transport, with gravity/pressure
terms balanced against actual open geometry. It must still pass the partial
free-surface, dam-break, trench momentum and sealed-pocket tests.

Current canonical `WaterState` stores one amount and one trapped ledger value
per coarse cell. Those cannot reconstruct which side of an internal wall held
water: summing two component amounts loses information. Before activation,
design a new explicitly versioned canonical state with geometry-scoped stable
keys and component amounts/ledger, atomically journaled with terrain. Define
old-save conversion without inventing within-cell allocation; reject ambiguous
states or use an explicit validated conversion. Presentation may aggregate
amounts but must remain separate from canonical recovery. No format change or
implicit migration is made in this increment.

Boundary/growth changes require remapping actual overlapping fine open space,
conservative displacement through matched portals and a trapped ledger when
capacity is insufficient. Immutable workers must build/solve from a consistent
terrain revision; only the owner installs a validated result at a tick boundary.
The 39 ms default-domain build cost also argues against an unconditional
owner-thread rebuild per edit. Preserve residency suspension and existing
source/outflow accounting. These remain required work, not completed claims.
