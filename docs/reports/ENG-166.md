# ENG-166 (W2): regions, sleep and threading

Status: implemented 2026-10-10 in `spall_fluid::columns`; headless CPU only. Not
wired into `AuthoritativeWater` (ENG-167). Design: `docs/water-agent-handoff.md`.
Builds on `docs/reports/ENG-165.md`.

## What exists

- **Sparse regions.** 32 x 32 columns (the brick footprint). A region is
  allocated only if it holds water or touches one that does. Terrain comes from
  a `TerrainSource` (implemented for a dense `SolidBoundary` and for a voxel
  `Volume`); the solver reads region masks brick by brick.
- **Active / asleep.** A region sleeps after 20 quiet steps (every pipe touching
  it below 1e-3 cells^3/s). A world with no active region returns from `step`
  immediately.
- **Wake rules.** Terrain edit (`refresh_regions` / `apply_boundary`: the region
  and its four neighbours), flow above the threshold across a border into a
  sleeping region, `add_water` / `fill_column_to` (sources), `wake_all`, and
  residency returning.
- **Frozen boundary.** A region whose terrain is not resident is never
  allocated (or, if already allocated, `freeze_regions` removes its pipes): a
  closed wall, never a drain. `notify_resident` allocates it, wakes both sides
  and water crosses.
- **Worldgen seeding at rest.** `seed_cells` takes the full water cells (e.g.
  `WaterPlan::cells`), allocates their regions plus a one-region ring, and
  leaves everything asleep. An idle lake therefore costs nothing.
- **Parallel stepping.** All passes (head relaxation, flow, outflow limit,
  apply, quiet tracking) run over the active span and pipe lists with Rayon
  from the previous state, applying together. Results are bit-identical at 1,
  2, 4 and 7 threads (test).
- **Incremental growth.** Allocating a ring region appends spans and pipes
  without touching existing indices or flows. Terrain refresh does a full
  rebuild and keeps the flow of surviving pipes.

## Tests (`cargo test --release -p spall_fluid --test columns --test column_regions`)

All 16 pass (the 9 W1 tests unchanged on the sparse implementation, plus 7 new):
sparse allocation and zero-cost rest; water spreading allocates regions with
volume conserved; quiet regions sleep and an edit then border flow wake the
neighbour (pools equalise through a one-cell gap in 220 s, sleep again, levels
equal within 0.05); border flow wakes a sleeping region; non-resident region is
a closed boundary that neither drains nor counts as empty, and water crosses
once resident; frozen region keeps its water; bit-identical sparse stepping
across thread counts.

The "dam break" is a stand-in: a 316 x 316 column slab of water filled to 20
cells above sea level, released over the middle of the generated world. It is
not the named dam-break scenario of W1 and does not model a breached dam wall.

**The 5 ms release target is not met.** Thread scaling at 1024 is 1 thread
80 ms, 4 threads 28 ms, 16 threads 15-17 ms, so the work is sound per element
(about 12 ns per span/pipe per substep) but memory-bound and about 6 substeps
per step. Raising the Courant factor to 1.0 gives 10 ms but breaks
conservation (drift 4e-7), so it is not adopted.

## Differences from the handoff

- **One global substep, not one per region.** All active regions step with the
  smallest stable dt (set by the deepest wetted pipe). Per-region substeps would
  cut shallow regions to 1-2 substeps and are the main remaining lever for the
  release target. Not implemented.
- Pressure ramp, overfill spring and f64 volumes carry over from ENG-165.

## Not covered / risks

- Terrain refresh and freeze rebuild all pipes (O(allocated spans)); not
  measured at the 4096 scale.
- `rebuild_lists` and quiet tracking are O(active) per step and partly serial.
- The state is not persisted or replicated here (ENG-167). Source/sink hooks
  are only `add_water`.
- Sleep thresholds (1e-3, 20 steps) are tuned on the test fixtures.
- Peak pipe speed reported in the release (up to ~70 m/s) is q/A at very thin
  wetted areas on steep drops, not a physical front speed.
- Hands-on play is unrun.

Next unblocked: ENG-167 (W3 production swap), or a per-region substep pass to
close the W2 release target first.

## Correction (2026-10-10, found in play): stability under a sustained drain

The settle-to-rest and dam-break tests above never ran water continuously on
deep real terrain. A play session that cut a shoreline and drained a 4096-world
lake showed stationary spikes and troughs; reproducing it (a sink patch in a
generated lake, `examples/sandbox/examples/water_cut_probe.rs`) showed the
simulation itself going unstable: head differences grew from 0.9 to about 30
cells, flows to several hundred, and single columns sat 25 cells off their
neighbours, with no overfill and identical results with sleep disabled. The
original defaults (safety factor 0.5, damping 1.5/s) sat on the stability
boundary on real terrain. Safety factors 0.35 to 0.4 are stable at the original
damping; 0.5 needed damping of 3 or more.

Defaults are now safety factor 0.35 and damping 2.0/s: stable for 180 s of
sustained drain on the real lake (worst spike near the drain 0.04 cells), at a
cost of about 15% more time per release step (p50 19.2 ms against 17.3 ms at
1024; the 5 ms target remains missed) and a slightly slower dam-break front
(peak 3.9 m/s, arrival 2.6 s, against 4.8 m/s and 2.3 s). Regression test:
`spall_fluid/tests/column_drain.rs` drains a deep bowl for 150 s and requires
the surface away from the drain to stay flat (0.002 cells; 19.7 with the old
defaults, kept as an ignored negative control) and volume to be conserved. Two
region tests now wait for the condition instead of a fixed time.
