# ENG-167 (W3): production swap to the column water

Status: **partly done, 2026-10-10.** The server's authoritative water, its
persistence, replication, client delivery and the sandbox scenes now use the
stacked-column model end to end and are tested. Three W3 acceptance items are
not met (below), so the ticket is not done. `grid_mac` and its tests are
untouched and still compile and pass; W5 deletes them.

## Gaps from the first pass, and how they closed (2026-10-11)

1. **Interactive play session: done.** The user ran the column water in a real
   client (look, drain stability, buoyancy, swimming, raft) and confirmed it.
2. **Server tick cost: measured; no worker mode built.** `water_tick_cost_probe`
   (4096 showcase, seed 7, release) cuts the biggest lake's shore and times
   every tick. A 10 m cut: fluid step p50 0.9 ms, p99 2.3 ms, max 2.7 ms;
   whole tick p50 1.7 ms; no fluid time skipped. A 40 m cut (5,028 columns
   remapped): at most 30 active regions, step p99 2.6 ms, none skipped. Resting
   water ticks at p50 1.1 ms. So realistic releases fit easily in a 16.7 ms
   tick. What did cost was a terrain edit near water: the refresh rebuilt every
   pipe in the world (2.4 M pipes at 830 regions), 105 to 143 ms per edit.
   Only the touched regions and the owners next to them are rebuilt now, the
   rest are carried over with shifted indices (flow included): about 60 ms
   (`columns.rs` `carry_pipes`; test: the incremental pipes equal a full
   rebuild, flow survives, volume is conserved; checked to fail if the index
   shift is wrong). The remaining ~60 ms is O(world) copying of the span
   arrays and rebuilding the adjacency; going lower needs per-region storage.
   Not measured: a flood with ~100 k columns all moving at once (the ENG-166
   bench put that at 12 to 17 ms per step). That is the one case where a worker
   mode would matter; stepping stays capped at 2 per tick with the debt
   reported. The edit tick's other ~70 ms (and a ~500 ms first tick) is the
   terrain edit and warm-up, not water.
3. **Interest management: done for regional clients.** A session that streams
   terrain by region (`region_radius`) is sent only water within
   `region_radius + 1` regions of its player (`WaterInterest`); a region is taken
   up inside that radius and dropped (empty baselines, so the client forgets it)
   only beyond +2 more, so pacing at the edge does not flap. Non-regional
   clients still get all water. Tests: only nearby water is sent and a moving
   player gets the new region and drops the old; hysteresis at the edge.
   Not covered: an end-to-end regional client with water (the networked test
   uses a full-baseline client), so `water_interest`'s radius and position
   wiring in `serve.rs` is untested beyond compiling.

## Other risks and limits

- A terrain edit still refreshes with a whole-world pipe rebuild
  (O(allocated spans)); the clone of the terrain volume per edit tick is also
  unmeasured at world scale.
- Journal deltas can lag flowing water by up to 20 ticks (an edit tick always
  journals). A crash during motion recovers the water as of the last delta.
- Water bounded by the end of resident terrain is a closed wall, so water in a
  scene with open edges collects at the resident boundary instead of
  draining; scenes that relied on the dense domain's closed box now rely on the
  resident envelope.
- `cargo xtask check` was not run.
- The uncommitted ENG-127 stopgap in `worldgen_scene.rs` and its test are
  untouched; the old dense path still uses them.

Next: ENG-169 (removal of the old dense path).

## Fluid step on its own thread (2026-10-11)

The first play sessions on the 4096 world showed stalls once a big lake moved:
with ~200 active regions the step took 5 ms on a quiet machine and 50 to 115 ms
whenever the client (meshing, rendering) was using every core, and a step that
late held up the whole server tick (snapshots 100 to 200 ms apart, hard
prediction corrections). Cutting the cost helped (steps sliced three ways, frames
and totals built once per step and in parallel, replication encoded under a
1.5 ms per-tick budget), but a parallel step on a loaded machine cannot be made
predictable, so the server now runs it on a worker thread:
`AuthoritativeColumnWater::start_worker` (`SimulationConfig::column_water_worker`,
on for the server, off for tests and tools, which keep the deterministic inline
step).

- The tick hands over the fluid time owed and reads the last published frame. It
  waits only for a terrain refresh, which needs a whole world, and a checkpoint,
  which needs the exact state; the periodic journal delta is simply late if a step
  is in flight.
- Players and floating bodies feel the published frame (surface, quantized
  current) through `ColumnWaterFrame::sampler`, the same data clients receive, so
  prediction and server agree better than before.
- A worker that panics is reported by the next tick as an error, not hidden.
- Cost of the worker: water lags rather than stalls when the machine is starved
  (fluid time beyond two steps per tick is reported as skipped).

Measured with `water_tick_cost_probe` (4096 world, 120 m cut, 12 busy-loop
processes on a 16-thread machine): tick p99 143 ms inline, 0.5 ms with the worker;
on a quiet machine tick p50 2.7 ms inline, 0.25 ms with the worker.
