# ENG-168 (W4): water rendering

Status: implemented 2026-10-10; seen in the real client on a generated world
(calm water) and in a synthetic GPU harness (disturbed water). **Not yet
confirmed by hands-on play with a spring or a breach.**

## Why

The first play check of the column water (ENG-167) reported two problems:
box outlines on the water, and chaotic jagged waves whenever the water was
disturbed; and the frame rate fell to ~15 fps after cutting a shoreline in a
4096 world. Causes found in the code:

- The old look meshed each 32 x 32 region on its own, so every region border
  had walls and a seam (the "boxes"), and shaded each triangle flat.
- Every cell's simulated height was drawn unsmoothed and replicated a few times
  a second, so cell-scale noise showed as jagged, jumping waves.
- Each publication cloned every region and rebuilt the whole mesh on the render
  thread by scanning dense frames, which is the frame-rate drop.

## What changed

- **One continuous sheet** (`spall_render`): the water is a vertex-less grid
  drawn from a camera-centred height field texture (`Rgba32Float`: top, bottom,
  smoothed height, agitation). The vertex stage displaces it from the smoothed
  heights, so there are no regions, seams or walls. The old mesh path stays only
  for the demos.
- **Eased, smoothed, incremental** (`spall_client::column_surface`): one
  persistent height tile per region; displayed heights glide to the server's
  (time constant 0.15 s); a separable 5-tap binomial kernel smooths across
  region borders; agitation (how fast the surface moves) is low-passed. Only
  changed tiles are recomposed and only their rectangle is uploaded
  (`update_water_field_rect`); the field is replaced whole only when the camera
  nears its edge. Updates are applied per replicated window, not per region.
- **New look** (`fs_water_grid`): smooth normals, depth-based colour (clear
  shallows, deep teal), fresnel sky reflection with a sharp sun glint, gentle
  continuous ripples (stronger where agitated), soft shorelines (the sheet fades
  out by depth and over the last cell), a thin ragged shore-foam band, and foam
  patches only on water that is moving hard. The underwater fog, tint and
  caustics are unchanged.

## Measured

- **Real client, generated world** (`cargo xtask play --release --worldgen
  showcase --seed 7 --shots ...`): 60 fps, frame 16.6-18 ms, no rejected water
  updates; overhead and low-angle shots show a single smooth lake with clean
  shores.
- **Surface cost, worst case** (whole 128 m field, 256 tiles, every tile
  changing at the replication rate; ignored test
  `a_whole_field_of_moving_water_stays_cheap_per_frame`, release): per-frame
  `advance` mean 3.4 ms, worst 9 ms; replication tick 2.5 ms on the network
  thread. It was 9 ms and 10 ms before making the kernel separable and not
  smoothing twice. Normal play is far below this.
- Tests: 8 `column_surface` unit tests (spans to field, smoothing reach and
  border continuity, easing, agitation, dirty rectangles, recentring), plus the
  ignored GPU test `water_grid_gpu` that writes `.local/runs/water-grid/*.png`.
  468 tests pass over spall_render, spall_client, spall_protocol and
  spall_server; clippy clean.

## Not covered

- A disturbed surface was only rendered in the synthetic harness; the in-game
  tour could not trigger a spring or a breach. The ripple and foam strengths
  (`amount`, the agitation scale of 0.9 m/s) are tuned on that harness.
- Water stacked under other water draws only the highest surface.
- Two other ENG-167 gaps remain: server tick cost under a large release and
  interest management.
- Tuning knobs: `EASE_S`, `AGITATION_FULL_M_S`, `KERNEL` in `column_surface.rs`;
  the colour and foam constants in `fs_water_grid`.

## First play feedback (2026-10-10) and fix

Two screenshots from a shoreline cut and drain showed a white foam strip that
flashed down equal-sized strips of the lake, and stationary rippled "troughs".
Cause, a bug in this work: agitation is the speed of a column's surface between
two server updates, but updates arrive per 128-column window and the interval
was tracked per region, so every window after the first in a tick got a
near-zero interval (clamped to 0.05 s) and a slow drain read as a surge. While
water kept draining, each update re-raised agitation, so the rippled band stayed
put. Fixed: the interval is per window (minimum 0.1 s), speeds under 0.15 m/s
count as settling, and water appearing in a shallow column no longer starts
agitated. Tests: a slow drain delivered window by window stays calm; a real
surge is agitated in every window. Not verified in play: whether any of the
troughs are real simulation depressions near the cut (a drain does pull the
surface down toward it), which would remain.

## Second play feedback (2026-10-10): wet-looking dry ground, striped lakebed

1. Draining left "water shader" on newly dry ground. A drained area keeps a few
   millimetres of unmoving film (the simulation stops films under 1/64 cell and
   the server publishes anything quantizing above zero), and the terrain shader
   treated any water at all as "underwater": fog, tint, caustics. Fix: water
   thinner than 4 cm is not water for terrain shading (`WATER_MIN_DEPTH_M`).
2. The lakebed seen from above showed fine scan-line stripes and moire, which
   looked like z-fighting. It was not depth fighting: the underwater test
   `p.y < bottom` compared the bed surface (exactly at the pool's bottom height)
   with itself, so floating-point error switched fog on and off per pixel.
   Fix: a 3 cm margin (`WATER_BOTTOM_TOL_M`) at both comparisons. Also removed
   the 0.25 m snapping of the fog path, made caustics continuous in space and
   time and faded with distance, and fogged against the smoothed surface instead
   of the raw per-cell height (which jitters by hundredths of a metre).
   Found by reproducing it in a terraced-bed GPU harness (`the_lakebed_seen_from_above`):
   the bed alone was clean, the bed under water was striped; both fixes were
   checked there (a film region renders as plain dry sand) and in the real client.


## Floating bodies (2026-10-10)

Bodies previously ignored water. `SimWorld::apply_buoyancy` (spall_sim,
`world/buoyancy.rs`) now runs each tick before the physics step: each dynamic
body's solid cells are sampled (strided above 2048 cells, cached per volume
stamp); a sample below the surface of its column's water span
(`ColumnWorld::surface_at_m`) displaces its cell volume in proportion to how far
under it is; the displaced volume gives an upward impulse `rho g V dt` at the
centre of the displaced volume (so a lopsided load tilts the raft), and the
submerged fraction damps linear (1.2/s) and angular (2.0/s) velocity. It is a
one-way coupling: bodies do not push the water. A body displaces water with its
solid cells only, so a sealed hull must be solid to float.

Tests (`spall_sim/tests/buoyancy.rs`, headless): a 4 m x 3 m x 0.5 m wood raft
(700 kg/m3) floats flat with its base at 2.652 m (theory 2.65) and stays within
2 cm of that; stone sinks; a raft dropped from 2.5 m settles; a stone block on
one end tilts it. On the generated showcase lake (`the_lake_floaters_...`) the
raft, three 1 m crates (250 kg/m3), a log and a stone block behave as their
densities say. A wood *cube* (700 kg/m3) correctly floats rolled onto an edge,
tilted about 50 degrees: cubes between about 26% and 74% of water's density have
no stable flat orientation, so it is a poor test object (and the mistake of
passing a raft's dimensions in the wrong order, giving a standing slab that
tipped over, cost a debugging detour).

Launch: `--spawn-raft` (with `--worldgen`) places the six objects in open water
at least 1 m deep, the nearest such spot to where `--spawn-at-lake` stands.

Not covered: players standing on or pushing a raft is untried; bodies are not
woken when water arrives under a sleeping body that is not already touching it;
no flowing-water drag (a current does not push a raft yet); the bodies' densities
are re-derived from material densities after a save and restore, not kept.
