## Increment 1 — separated regions, multi-region collapse, late join

Date: 2026-09-10. Branch `feat/eng-30-t23-inc1-separated-regions`.

Adds the bounded-world integrated-acceptance scene and drives the first slice
of the G3 workload — geographically separated players, a multi-region collapse,
and a late join after heavy edits — through the existing multi-process harness.

### Scene: `separated-regions`

`spall_voxel::fixtures::separated_regions_scene` /
`spall_sim::fixtures::separated_regions_setup`, served as
`Scene::SeparatedRegions` (`--scene separated-regions` | `t23-g3` | `g3`).

- One volume **bounded to `256 x 128 x 256 m`** (`32 x 16 x 32` bricks at
  `CellSizeCode::Quarter`) — the G3 operating envelope.
- Two independent collapsible bridge structures: a **west** region at the world
  origin and an **east** region offset by `+72, +0, +72` cells (`+18 m` on `x`
  and `z`). Each is an anchored floor + one slender column + a raised beam the
  column alone holds up. Cutting one region's column detaches only that
  region's beam.
- Only the two regions plus the ground between them are resident, so the
  terrain occupancy grid and every collider rebuild stay CPU-cheap despite the
  large world box.
- Every connecting client gets an authoritative player capsule
  (`Scene::has_players`); `SEPARATED_REGION_SPAWNS` puts **even slots in the
  west region and odd slots in the east region**, so connected players start
  geographically separated. Per-scene spawn tables replace the previous
  unconditional `WALK_ARENA_SPAWNS` (`Scene::player_spawns`).

CPU proof: `cargo test -p spall_sim --test separated_regions` —
`cutting_one_region_leaves_the_other_region_intact` (one column cut detaches
exactly one beam; cutting the other column detaches a second, independent beam;
the two beams' collider regions are one per region; occupied-cell conservation
holds across both cuts) and `both_detached_beams_come_to_rest_on_their_own_floor`
(both freed beams sleep at rest near their floor, neither falls through, the
terrain floors survive).

### Multi-process run: `fixtures/scenarios/t23-g3.json`

`cargo xtask scenario --name t23-g3` launches one `sandbox-server` and four
`sandbox-client` OS processes over real QUIC (optionally behind per-client UDP
loss proxies). Clients 0 and 2 spawn in the west region, client 1 in the east
region. Client 0 severs the west column (~tick 40) and client 1 severs the east
column (~tick 100) — a multi-region collapse detaching one beam per region.
Clients then excavate each region's floor **ends** (outside the beam x-spans, so
each beam keeps a floor to rest on). Client 3 is a `--late-join` replica that
connects after those six edits, pulls a dependency-complete baseline over a
real QUIC bulk transfer, drains the catch-up queue, and lands one cut of its
own.

The harness passes only when: every one of the seven scripted cuts commits; the
server and all four clients agree on the final canonical topology hash; the
committed topology-event stream replayed from the tick-0 baseline
(`sandbox-server --replay`) reproduces that hash; and every detached body is
reported at rest (`require_body_settled`).

## Increment 2 — cold restart and recovery round-trip

Date: 2026-09-10. Same branch.

Adds a `restart_check` flag to the scenario harness and turns it on for
`t23-g3`. After the normal run and the exact-replay check, the harness:

1. stops the run's server (which writes a shutdown checkpoint), then
2. launches a **fresh** `sandbox-server --serve --save` over the same
   `world.db` — a cold recovery from the shutdown checkpoint plus the durable
   journal, with **no client edit replay from world creation** — and requires
   its recovered `final_world_hash` to equal the agreed hash, then
3. connects a fresh `--late-join` client to the restarted server and requires
   it to converge to that same hash: the recovered world serves a correct
   baseline.

All three are gate requirements (`requirements_met` fails otherwise), and the
run's `summary.json` gains `restart_checked`,
`restart_recovered_hash_matches`, `restart_reconnect_hash_matches`, and
`restart_recovered_world_hash`.

#### Measured

| Run | Recovered server | Late-join reconnect | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| loopback, no loss | `passed`, 300 ticks, 0 committed, hash `cac2893c…d18e6de` = agreed | `passed`, 0 applied, 288 motion, hash `cac2893c…d18e6de` = agreed | passed |
| main run at `--loss-percent 3` | hash = agreed | hash = agreed | passed |

The recovered server commits nothing (no edits to make) — it only has to come
up and serve one late-join baseline of the post-collapse world. The reconnecting
client applies zero transactions and still converges, because a `--late-join`
replica pulls the full current baseline rather than replaying edits.

`world_hash` is topology (cells + ownership), not float poses, so the recovered
hash is exact across the restart despite physics re-initialisation.

## Increment 4 — impaired late join: bounded explicit failure

Date: 2026-09-10. Same branch.

`docs/validation.md` G3: *"Retry/catch-up stress must terminate with either
successful join or a bounded explicit failure while connected clients
continue."* Increment 1's `t23-g3` already covers the **successful-join** half
(clean and at 3% loss). This increment covers the **bounded-failure** half with
an explicit assertion, and makes a failed late join distinguishable from a hang
or a silent partial state.

- `sandbox-client`: a `--late-join` replica that cannot obtain a baseline now
  writes a structured `{ "result": "join-failed", … }` summary and exits with a
  distinct code (`4`) instead of exiting with no summary. It was already
  *prompt* — `perform_late_join` returns on the first failed transfer, the
  server owns the retry budget — this makes the outcome *legible*.
- `session.rs`: a new `late_join_may_fail` scenario flag. When set, a
  `late_join_clients` replica is accepted if it **either** converged to the
  agreed hash **or** ended in a bounded explicit failure — a `join-failed`
  summary **and** a real process exit (`exit_code.is_some()`; a deadline kill is
  `None` and is rejected as a hang). The live (non-late) clients and the server
  must still agree on one hash and the committed stream must still replay to it.
  `summary.json` gains `impaired_late_join_bounded_failure`.
- `fixtures/scenarios/t23-g3-impaired-join.json`: three live clients run the
  two-region collapse and converge, the run reaches body-settle quiescence, and
  the server shuts down; client 3's `--late-join` connect is delayed to `12 s`,
  well past that shutdown, so its baseline transfer cannot start. Deterministic
  — no reliance on a particular packet-loss draw.

#### Measured

```
sandbox-client: passed  ... hash=c2d5047d…afe7   (x3 live)
sandbox-server: passed ticks=287 clients=3 committed=6 hash=c2d5047d…afe7
sandbox-server: replay passed topology_events=6 hash=c2d5047d…afe7
sandbox-client: transport: timed out after 5s          (client 3, late)
session passed
```

`summary.json`: `result: passed`, `impaired_late_join_bounded_failure: true`,
`all_hashes_match: true`, `requirements_met: true`; the `per_client` row for
client 3 is `result: "join-failed"`, `hash_matches_server: false`, and
`client3.summary.json` carries `"result": "join-failed"`, `"connected": false`,
`"detail": "transport: timed out after 5s"`. Exit code `4`.

`t23-g3` (the successful-join half) still passes unchanged after this change.

## Increment 7 — transaction + structural correctness on the logical view (slice B)

Date: 2026-09-10. Same branch.

The commit path (`spall_sim::commit` parent `result_hash`), the replica's
candidate-hash validator (`spall_client::replica`), whole-world conservation
(`SimWorld::total_solid_cells`), and staging (`stage_edit` `pre_solid` /
`post_solid`) all read resident-only state. Slice B makes every one of them use
the **logical** brick set, and makes an edit that needs geometry it cannot see
a hard error rather than a silent corruption.

- `SimWorld` and `ReplicaWorld` carry an optional per-volume `EvictedBricks`
  (empty by default → byte-identical to today; `world_hash` folds
  `canonical_logical_volume_for`). `SimWorld::evict_brick` /
  `clear_evicted_after_reload` implement the evict / reload digest transitions;
  a full baseline clears the namespace on both sides.
- The parent transaction `result_hash` and the replica's per-`result_hash`
  candidate check fold resident ∪ evicted digests, so a server and a replica
  that have **different** bricks evicted still agree and converge.
- Conservation (`total_solid_cells`, staging `pre_solid` + `post_solid`) is over
  the logical solid count — exact no matter where the evicted bricks are.
- `stage_edit` returns `StageError::EvictedGeometryRequired` (snapshot
  untouched) when the edit writes, structurally borders (its touched bricks and
  detached components, grown one brick), or — at commit —
  `CommitError::EvictedGeometryRequired` when the collider rebuild would sample
  an evicted brick. A far, edit-irrelevant eviction is left alone.

#### Measured

`cargo test -p spall_sim --test logical_commit` (4) — evicting an untouched
terrain brick moves neither the parent `result_hash`, `world_hash`, nor
`total_solid_cells`; hash + conservation stable across a non-canonical eviction
order; a cut whose support path runs through an evicted brick is refused with
the snapshot untouched; a cut clear of the evicted brick still stages and
balances its ledger. `cargo test -p spall_client --test logical_residency` (3) —
a replica that has evicted clean bricks still `Published`-validates a
full-server transaction and converges; the server's reported `world_hash` is
unchanged before/after it evicts a clean brick. `cargo fmt --all --check`;
`cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --all-features -- -D warnings`;
`cargo test --workspace --all-features` — green (the `spall_net` two-process
QUIC test is the known flake; passes on rerun).

Deferred to slice C: dependency-complete reload-and-retry so a far,
edit-irrelevant eviction stops blocking a commit; the `KnownEmpty` membership
rule. Residency stays default-off.

## Increment 8 — backing + capture (slice C)

Date: 2026-09-10. Same branch.

Makes residency **usable**: an edit that needs an evicted brick's cells reloads
it, and the late-join / repair capture paths serve a complete world from an
evicted server.

- `spall_sim::BrickBacking` trait + `MemoryBacking` (durable source of brick
  geometry). `SimWorld::set_backing` + `reload_brick` / `reload_bricks` — load
  from the backing, reinstall, `verify_reload` against the retained digest, drop
  the digest. A `KnownEmpty` result installs an air brick at the retained
  revision (adds no new canonical brick).
- The edit pipeline catches `EvictedGeometryRequired` from **stage and
  commit**, reloads the named bricks, and re-stages the intent next tick. No
  backing, or an `Unavailable` record → a bounded explicit
  `evicted geometry unavailable` rejection with nothing mutated.
- `spall_server::logical_world_baseline` / `logical_capture_transfer` /
  `logical_brick_repair_patch` fill evicted bricks from the backing, so a late
  joiner or a one-brick repair still reaches the exact canonical hash.
  `world_baseline` / `brick_repair_patch` are the `backing = None` cases,
  byte-unchanged when nothing is evicted.

#### Measured

`cargo test -p spall_sim --test logical_reload` (3) — a seam cut whose region
needs an evicted brick reloads it and commits to the same `world_hash` as a
fully resident run; with no backing it is rejected cleanly and the world is
untouched; a wrong backing record is refused by `verify_reload` and keeps the
digest. `cargo test -p spall_server --test logical_baseline` (3) — a late-join
baseline over an evicted server reconstructs the full topology on a fresh
replica (`world_hash` equal); a repair patch covers an evicted brick at the
right revision; `world_baseline` is byte-unchanged with no evictions.
`cargo fmt --all --check`; `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets
--all-features -- -D warnings`; `cargo test --workspace --all-features` — green.

Deferred to slice D: routing `ResidencyController::enforce_budget` through
`SimWorld::evict_brick`, bridging the durable store to `BrickBacking`, and
checkpoint capture via `ResidencyController::capture_checkpoint` in the serve
loop. Residency stays default-off.

## Increment 9 — default-off serve-loop residency wiring (slice D)

Date: 2026-09-10. Same branch.

Wires the logical-view machinery (slices A–C) into `serve()` behind a
**default-off** knob, so a run can actually evict terrain bricks mid-session and
still converge / replay / restart to the byte-identical hash.

- `ServeConfig.residency: Option<ResidencyLimits>` (`None` → every prior run is
  byte-unchanged). `sandbox-server --residency-budget-bricks N`
  [`--residency-radius-bricks R`]; `session.rs` forwards
  `residency_budget_bricks` / `residency_radius_bricks` from a scenario file.
- `spall_server::residency_pass::ResidencyPass`: owns an `Arc<MemoryBacking>`
  seeded from the terrain and installed on `SimWorld`. Each tick after commits it
  keeps a Chebyshev brick box around every player capsule resident and evicts
  out-of-interest terrain after `EVICT_SETTLE_TICKS` consecutive out-of-interest
  ticks (hysteresis so it does not thrash against the edit pipeline's
  reload-and-retry). `on_commit` refreshes the backing for the bricks an edit
  touched; `reload_all` runs before every periodic **and** the shutdown
  checkpoint so `persist::capture` always snapshots the whole world.
- Late-join baseline and brick-repair capture now call
  `baseline::logical_capture_transfer` / `logical_brick_repair_patch` with the
  pass's backing (`LateJoin.backing`), so a joiner or a repair over an evicted
  server still reconstructs the exact canonical hash.
- `spall_sim::commit`: a collider-rebuild `ExtractError::Unresident` now returns
  **every** evicted brick in the volume (not the single named cell's brick), so
  one reload makes the candidate's whole bounding box resident and the retry
  commits the next tick instead of the residency pass re-evicting an
  already-reloaded brick before the set is whole.
- `ServeSummary` bumped to **version 4**: `residency_evictions_total`,
  `residency_reloads_total`, `resident_terrain_bricks_{min,max,final}`,
  `residency_budget_miss_ticks` (all `0` when residency is off).

and `[92, 1, 73]`, `radius: 8`) request a sphere whose centre sits only 1 cell
above the world's `y = 0` (and, for the west blast, `z = 0`) floor. An

production `resolve_intent` path), so the rejection is 100% deterministic on

it. Fixed by moving both blast centres to `y = 12` (and the west blast's `z`
to `12`) — comfortably inside bounds (sphere floor at cell `4`) while staying
within the populated region.

effect, but corrupts exactly the kind of per-client diagnostic this increment
added. Fixed: a new `submitted_by: HashMap<RequestId, SessionId>` records who
staged each request; the tick-report loop now `send_to`s the resolved status

no entry, which should not happen).

### Row 15 — root-caused to `rapier3d`, mitigation confirmed, regression pinned

doesn't respond to horizontal input for hundreds of ticks" and suspected

### Row 16 — fixed: `distance_travelled_m` now reads authoritative, not predicted

Root cause matched the reported symptom exactly once traced through
`spall_client::predict`: `PlayerMovementSummary::distance_travelled_m` was
computed from `self.predicted`, but the mover only advances prediction while
`ClientPhysics::covers` holds (increment 16) — a residency reload round trip
legitimately *holds* (does not advance) predicted for a while, the same
mechanism the adjacent `at_rest` check in `spall_client::net` already
accounts for by reading `authoritative()` instead. If a run's final summary
happened to be captured while predicted was held but authoritative had
already progressed further, the reported distance under-reported a run that
converged correctly everywhere else — exactly "the predictor's own
bookkeeping was the casualty, not the simulation" from PR #99's report.
Fixed: `summary()` now computes `distance_travelled_m` from
`self.authoritative`, which "keeps updating from every snapshot regardless"
(same reasoning as the existing `at_rest` check) and never has this gap.
`max_distance_from_start_m` (a running max, used for out-and-back proofs)
was already immune to this — it never regresses — so it is unchanged.

**New, separate discovery while verifying the fix** (not row 16 itself, not
fixed here): four runs of `t23-g3-full-envelope` (2 failed, 2 passed) showed
the fix working correctly where it applied — `distance_travelled_m` was
sane in every run — but one failure was a *different* gate,
`held_button_release_ok: false`, with the player's final
predicted/authoritative position around `y = -80 m` (fallen through the
world after the scripted traversal, off the far edge of the causeway). This
is the T19 "a lost button-up cannot leave it walking forever" acceptance
bullet, not row 16's predictor-staleness mechanism — a client that does not
release its held movement input at the end of a scripted leg can walk off
the far edge into the void. Not diagnosed further or fixed here.

### Row 10 follow-up — the server-side retry/catch-up-exhaustion mechanism is proven; a real client/protocol gap was found alongside it, not fixed

`t23-g3-impaired-join.json` already proves row 10's "bounded explicit
failure" half for a late joiner that never gets a baseline at all (connect
delayed past server shutdown). The open half was "retry/catch-up exhaustion
while connected clients stay active" — nobody had built a scenario that
actually drives a joining client's catch-up queue past `catch_up_cap`
repeatedly while other clients keep committing. Built one
(`fixtures/scenarios/t23-g3-retry-exhaustion.json`) and its supporting
harness wiring:
- `tools/xtask/src/session.rs` gained a `retry_exhaustion` scenario field
  (mirroring `join_budget`'s existing "shape only the named client" proxy
  pattern) that also passes `--catch-up-cap` / `--max-join-retries` through
  to `sandbox-server` (both CLI flags already existed on `ServeConfig`; the
  scenario harness had never wired either).
- The fixture: clients 0–2 commit 24 tightly-spaced cuts (client 3 owns none
  of them) while client 3 late-joins immediately behind a shaped proxy with
  `catch_up_cap: 1`.

**Calibration finding**: the shaped proxy governs the *entire* connection,
not just the bulk baseline transfer, so bandwidth/loss low enough to
guarantee queue overflow (initially tried: 128–1000 B/s) also blows the
unrelated 5 s QUIC handshake timeout before the client ever reaches
`Phase::Joining` at all (`late_join_retries` stayed `0` — it never even
registered as joining). Needed a much higher bandwidth (50,000 B/s, proven
to let the handshake complete) and a very tight, front-loaded edit cadence
(24 commits starting at tick 10, 8 ticks apart) instead, so multiple commits
land inside every catch-up window regardless of transfer speed.

**Verified working as designed**: `server.summary.json` shows
`late_join_retries: 2`, `late_joins_failed: 1` — `LateJoin::fan_out_transaction`'s
existing recapture-on-overflow path fires repeatedly and correctly gives up
once `max_join_retries` is exceeded, exactly as increment 11's original
design intended. This is real, previously-unexercised proof that the
**server-side** half of "retry/catch-up stress must terminate with... a
bounded explicit failure" (`docs/validation.md`) works.

**Genuine gap found, not fixed**: the run still reports `session FAILED` —
not because the server behaved wrongly, but because the *disconnected
client* does not turn a mid-catch-up `Outbound::Shutdown` into the bounded
`"join-failed"` exit `late_join_may_fail` expects. Two compounding causes,
both in shared code far outside this scenario's scope to patch safely here:
1. `Outbound::Shutdown`'s `Bye` reason is the literal string `"server
   complete"` whether the run ended normally *or* a client's retries were
   exhausted (`crates/spall_server/src/serve.rs`) — indistinguishable at the
   source.
2. Even if the reason were distinct, nothing could currently read it:
   `spall_net::conn`'s control-stream reader discards a received `Bye`'s
   reason unconditionally (`NetMessage::Bye { .. } => return Ok(None)`) —
   there is no wire-level signal a client could act on today.
3. Separately, `spall_client::net`'s late-join `progressed` check
   (`config.late_join && baseline_bricks > 0`) is satisfied by the *first*
   baseline the client ever installed, before any catch-up drama — so a
   client dropped mid-exhaustion still reports `result: "passed"` with a
   stale, non-converged hash instead of failing explicitly. A silently wrong
   "passed" is a worse outcome than the hang this row exists to bound.

This needs a deliberate wire-protocol change (a distinguishable Shutdown
reason, actually surfaced past the transport layer) plus a client-side fix,
not a rushed patch alongside a test-authoring pass — filed as open, same
treatment as rows 15/16 above. The fixture is committed as an accurate,
reproducible demonstration of both the working server mechanism and the gap
— **a passing run of it should not be expected**, and one should not be read
as proof retry/catch-up exhaustion is fully handled. Read
`server.summary.json`'s `late_join_retries` / `late_joins_failed` (non-zero)
as the proof the server half already is.

## Increment 21 — row 10 closed: the client/protocol gap, fixed

Closes the client/protocol gap increment 20 found and left open. Three fixes,
all in shared transport/client code, ordered as they were found:

1. **`spall_net::conn::Connection`** now records the `reason` of a received
   `Bye` (`bye_reason()`), instead of discarding it in
   `NetMessage::Bye { .. } => return Ok(None)`. `Outbound::Shutdown` in
   `spall_server::serve` gained a `&'static str` reason — the ordinary
   end-of-run broadcast still says `Connection::BYE_REASON_COMPLETE`
   ("server complete"), but every place `LateJoin` gives up on a joining
   client (retry budget exceeded, or a re-capture failure) now says
   `Connection::BYE_REASON_CATCH_UP_EXHAUSTED` ("catch-up exhausted") instead
   — the two are now distinguishable on the wire, closing gap 1 from
   increment 20's writeup.
2. **A second, deeper bug surfaced while proving fix 1 with a targeted
   `spall_net` transport test**: `say_bye()` only calls `finish()` on the
   control send stream, which stops *sending* but does not wait for
   delivery; every caller (the server's writer task, and the client's own
   end-of-session path) calls `conn.close(..)` immediately afterward, which
   tears down the whole QUIC connection. A fresh test reproduced the
   consequence directly:
   `crates/spall_net/tests/transport.rs::bye_reason_survives_an_immediate_close_after_say_bye`
   failed with `Frame(Stream("connection lost"))` before the fix — the
   `Bye` never arrived at all, so the peer's `recv_record` saw an `Err`
   (connection reset), not the `Ok(None)` a graceful goodbye is supposed to
   produce. This is why increment 20's fixture reported `session FAILED`
   with the server's give-up counters already non-zero: the wire-level
   signal gap 1 (above) fixes assumed a delivered `Bye` in the first place,
   and the transport was racing it away before the reason could matter.
   Fixed in `say_bye()` itself (benefits every caller, not just this one):
   after `finish()`, wait (bounded, 2 s) on the send stream's `stopped()`
   future — which resolves once the peer has acknowledged receipt — before
   returning. The same test now passes in 0.01 s; the ordinary shutdown path
   (`t23-g3`, `t23-g3-loss`) shows no observable slowdown (`stopped()`
   resolves as soon as the ack arrives, well under the bound).
3. **`spall_client::net`**: the control reader now captures
   `conn.bye_reason()` into a new `Counters::disconnect_reason` when its
   record loop ends with `Ok(None)`. `run_async`'s summary construction
   treats `late_join && disconnect_reason == BYE_REASON_CATCH_UP_EXHAUSTED`
   as a bounded, explicit join failure (`result: "join-failed"`) — overriding
   the `progressed` check that increment 20 found was too lenient (satisfied
   by the *first* baseline installed, before the catch-up drama). Closes gap
   3 from increment 20's writeup. `examples/sandbox/src/bin/sandbox-client.rs`
   now exits code `4` for this `Ok(summary)` path too, matching the existing
   pre-baseline-handshake failure's exit code (previously only reachable via
   the `Err` branch).

`fixtures/scenarios/t23-g3-retry-exhaustion.json` (increment 20, previously
documented as expected to fail) now passes: client 3 reports
`result: "join-failed"` with a real exit, `server.summary.json` still shows
`late_join_retries: 2` / `late_joins_failed: 1`, and the session accepts it
under `late_join_may_fail` — `impaired_late_join_bounded_failure: true`,
`requirements_met: true`, `all_hashes_match: true`, overall `session passed`.
The pre-existing pre-baseline-handshake path (`t23-g3-impaired-join`) and the
ordinary happy-path scenarios (`t23-g3`, `t23-g4-workload`) are unaffected.

New regression coverage: `spall_server::serve::tests::catch_up_exhaustion_gives_the_dropped_joiner_a_distinguishable_bye_reason`
(registers a real `OutboundHandle` and asserts the queued `Shutdown` reason);

## Increment 22 — row 7 follow-up: bounded checkpoint capture, plus a real crash bug found

Closes the "full reload before checkpoint" half of the post-merge review's
P2/P3 durable-backing/bounded-capture complaint. Stacked on increment 21.

**The fix:** every periodic checkpoint used to call `ResidencyPass::reload_all`
— reinstall every currently-evicted terrain brick into the live world —
purely so `persist::capture`'s resident-only brick walk would see it, then let
the pass re-evict it over the following ticks. This defeated residency's whole
point at checkpoint time: live memory spiked back to the full world on every
single periodic checkpoint, not just at shutdown. `world_hash` was never at
risk (it has been the logical resident∪evicted-digest hash since increment 6
regardless of what `persist::capture`'s `bricks` list contains), but the
*checkpoint's own bricks* need to actually carry the evicted geometry or a
crash-recovery rebuild from that checkpoint would be missing it.

`ResidencyPass::capture_checkpoint` (`crates/spall_server/src/residency_pass.rs`)
replaces that: it calls the ordinary `persist::capture` for the live/resident
state (unchanged), then folds each currently-evicted terrain brick's durable
record — read straight from the pass's backing (`BrickBacking::load`), never
reinstalled into the live `Volume` — into the checkpoint's `bricks` list. An
evicted brick with no durable record (`BackingBrick::Unavailable`) fails the
whole capture (`PersistError::EvictedBrickUnavailable`) rather than silently
publishing a checkpoint whose `world_hash` claims geometry its `bricks` do not
actually carry — recovery's existing rebuilt-hash check would already catch
that mismatch, but failing at capture time is the earlier, clearer signal.
`serve.rs`'s two call sites (the periodic in-run checkpoint and the final
shutdown snapshot) both use it now; `ResidencyPass::reload_all` had no
remaining caller and was removed.

**A real bug found and fixed along the way:** proving this against the actual
`t23-g3-residency` scenario (not just a unit test) hit a genuine panic —
`background snapshots require resident geometry` — in
`spall_server::baseline::snapshot_world`, the background/worker-thread

## Increment 23 — row 2's `t23-g3-full-envelope` flake: real root cause, real fix

2026-09-13. `t23-g3-full-envelope` (increment 15 / PR #99) was never actually
reliable — it just wasn't run enough times in a row for that to show. Repro:
6 clean, un-lossy, repeated runs on this machine failed 4/6, always
`held_button_release_ok: false` with the scripted walker's final position deep
underground (`y` around `-60` to `-80`) — the T19 "walked off the world" gate.

**First hypothesis, wrong**: the scripted leg's own release-to-neutral timing
(`active_script_tick` in `spall_client::net`'s mover loop) is paced off the
*observed* server tick (`counters.last_tick`), not wall-clock time, and that
observation is read from a separate async task that could plausibly lag under
real OS scheduling contention (this harness runs the server and every client
as five real, separate processes). Two mitigations went in on this theory: a
`120`-cell (`30 m`) landing apron past the east region's floor (`spall_voxel::
fixtures::SEPARATED_REGIONS_FAR_EAST_LANDING_MARGIN_CELLS`, kept — genuine
extra margin, harmless, the `resident_brick_count() <= 20` budget still
holds), and a wall-clock backstop on the script pacing itself (`real_tick_clock`
in the mover loop, **reverted** — see below). Neither fully closed the flake:
10 more clean runs with just the apron still failed 4/10, now landing safely
on the wider apron but at wildly varying, non-deterministic distances (`25 m`
to `36 m` past nominal).

**Real root cause, found by instrumenting `PredictedPlayer::new`'s creation
site**: `tools/xtask/src/session.rs`'s client-spawn loop `Command::spawn()`s
all of a scenario's client processes back-to-back with no synchronization
between them, but `spall_net::endpoint::NetServer::authenticate` assigns each
connection's `SessionId` (and therefore its slot, spawn point, and player
entity) strictly by **connection arrival order**
(`SessionLease::acquire`) — nothing in the join handshake lets a client
request a specific slot. A scenario's `player_paths` (which client index gets
the movement script), `cuts` (which index owns which edit), and
`Scene::player_spawns()`'s per-slot spawn table are all keyed to *this loop's
own launch index*, which is only the same thing as the slot the server hands
out if connections happen to complete in launch order — not guaranteed when
five real OS processes start and race to connect concurrently. Confirmed
directly: added a one-line debug print at `PredictedPlayer::new`'s call site
and caught a failing run where the *west-walker process* (launch index 0,
carrying the `--move` script) was handed `EntityId(…657)` — client 1's entity
— and so predicted a player that actually spawned at the **east** region
(`111, 1, 1`), not its own west spawn (`7, 1, 1`). The walker then walked its
same fixed `+x` script from the wrong starting point, landing wherever that
happened to put it relative to the nearest edge — explaining every previously
mysterious final position across every earlier "fix" attempt, including the
first (unmodified) failing runs from before this increment (a final `x` of
`117.44` with a `distance_travelled_m` of only `6.47 m` is exactly `110.99 m`
[client 1's spawn] `+ 6.47 m`, not a real `110 m` walk at all).

**Fix**: `tools/xtask/src/session.rs`'s client-spawn loop now staggers every
non-late-joining client's own connect attempt by `launch_index * 50 ms`
(`--connect-delay-ms`, an existing `sandbox-client` flag previously only used
for late joiners) — cheap insurance making connection-arrival order match
launch order deterministically, since the QUIC handshake + auth round trip
this races against is at most a few milliseconds on loopback. This is a
harness fix, not an engine fix: nothing about the protocol's slot-by-arrival
design is wrong on its own (a real deployment has no "launch index" to keep
faithful to in the first place), only the test harness's assumption that
launch order and connection order coincide.

**The wall-clock-backstop mitigation was reverted**: taking
`max(observed_tick_delta, real_wall_clock_tick_delta)` to advance
`active_script_tick` turned out to systematically bias the script clock to run
*fast* (local wall-clock ticking has no network round-trip baked into it,
unlike the observed server tick, so it tends to be the larger of the two even
in normal, unimpaired operation) — this broke `t23-g3-traversal` outright
(`requirements_met: false`, the scripted leg cut short at `7.27 m` instead of
the required `20 m` outbound), a regression caught by running the full
regression sweep below before trusting the "fix". Reverted in full; the slot
race was the actual bug, this was chasing a symptom.

**Verified**: `t23-g3-full-envelope` clean — `23/23` across three batches
(`8`, `15`, and one further confirmation run) after the slot-assignment fix,
versus `~35%` passing before it (`8/23` across the pre-fix batches). Full
regression sweep, all passing with hashes matching previously-recorded values:
`t23-g3` (×1), `t23-g3-traversal` (×3 clean, ×1 at `--loss-percent 2`),
`t23-g3-residency`, `t23-g3-join-budget`, `t23-g3-impaired-join`,
`t23-g3-retry-exhaustion`, `t23-g4-workload` (the heaviest stress on the
connect-stagger change, 8 clients). `cargo fmt --all --check`; `cargo clippy
--workspace --all-targets --all-features -- -D warnings`; `cargo test
--workspace --all-features` — `79` passed, `0` failed (even the usually-flaky
`spall_net::separate_process_transport` passed on this run — the known
intermittent two-process transport flake, unrelated to this scenario).

**Row 2 is now fully closed**, including the flake — not just the original
happy-path evidence from increment 15.

Picked up row 11's remaining open item: the join-budget harness (increment

"worst-case-sized" resident footprint, as flagged since increment 13 itself.

(increment 19) — comfortably under the 16 MiB ceiling on size alone — but
never under the actual row-11 network profile (1 MiB/s / 100 ms RTT / 20 ms

**A real, previously-unknown crash bug found and fixed.** The very first run
crashed the whole 8-client session: every client's connection failed with a
cascading `PoisonError` after one `tokio-rt-worker` thread panicked at
`quinn-proto-0.11.17/src/connection/datagrams.rs:47:22`:
`datagrams.outgoing.payload_bytes desynchronized`. Traced (not guessed) to a
known, already-fixed upstream bug:
[quinn-rs/quinn#2805](https://github.com/quinn-rs/quinn/issues/2805).
`Datagrams::send(drop = true)`'s outgoing-buffer eviction loop subtracts an
evicted datagram's size from `payload_bytes` **twice** — once inside
`DatagramBuffer::pop_front` (which already maintains the counter), and again
at the call site. Under sustained eviction — exactly what a bandwidth-shaped
connection carrying real per-tick `MotionSnapshot` datagram traffic produces
— the counter drifts below its true value and underflows; a release build
wraps it to a huge value, drains the whole queue, and panics on
`.expect("datagrams.outgoing.payload_bytes desynchronized")` while the
connection's mutex is held. Because quinn's endpoint-level state is shared
across every connection on one socket, that one poisoned mutex cascaded into
every other client's connection on the same server process — a real,
reachable crash for *any* sufficiently bandwidth-constrained client with
meaningful datagram traffic, not specific to this scenario or to T23.
Fixed upstream on the `0.11.x` branch
([quinn-rs/quinn#2806](https://github.com/quinn-rs/quinn/pull/2806), merged
2026-08-29, closing #2805) but not yet in a released crates.io version
(0.11.17 remains latest as of this writing, confirmed via `cargo info
quinn-proto`). Pinned via `[patch.crates-io]` in the workspace root
`Cargo.toml` to that merge commit
(`f650e0f213b01ea7bebd60023e5df7df65e5c2bd`) — a git dependency pull, which
required explicit user approval under this environment's sandbox (flagged as
"untrusted code integration"). Verified: `cargo test --workspace
--all-features` clean (1169 lines, zero failures, including
`spall_net::separate_process_transport` passing on this run); `cargo fmt
--all --check`; `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --all-features -- -D
warnings` clean. Drop this patch once a quinn-proto release containing the
fix ships and `cargo update -p quinn-proto` picks it up.

**Row 11 still not closed: a second, real, well-diagnosed gap found after
the crash was fixed.** With the crash fixed, every re-run of
`t23-g4-join-budget` still ends `session FAILED`, `requirements_met: false` —
client 7 (the shaped client) reports `result: "join-failed"`,
`measured_baseline_compressed_bytes: 0`, `measured_ready_ms: 0`. Diagnosed by
instrumenting the client's own wait loop (temporarily; reverted after
diagnosis) rather than guessing further: the connection is alive and
healthy, but sits waiting for `BaselineBegin` for **33.16 s** before the
underlying QUIC stream itself reports `Frame(Stream("connection lost"))` —
the transport's own 30 s idle timeout (`TransportConfig::default().idle_timeout`,

late-join baseline capture spawns its own dedicated OS thread
(`spall_server::serve::LateJoin::on_baseline_ack`, `std::thread::spawn`) —
not a serialized queue — but with 7 other clients' capture threads already
contending for real CPU time (the same 20-25 s contention `t23-g4-workload`
already measured with zero network shaping involved), client 7's own capture
thread takes long enough under that contention that its connection's 30 s
idle window elapses first. The session-level protection that exists for
exactly this situation (`serve.rs`'s quiescence check: "a client still
mid-baseline (`lj.any_joining()`) also keeps the run alive... under an
impaired transport its bulk transfer can legitimately take longer than
quiescence") only protects a client the server has already recognized as
joining — it does not, and cannot, extend the QUIC transport's own
per-connection idle timeout, which is a separate, lower-level bound.

Tuning the late-join connect delay does not resolve this cleanly, and
splits into two failure modes instead of one: connecting near the other
seven clients (the shipped fixture, `late_join_connect_delay_ms: 200`

rest and it loses to the idle timeout, as described above. Delaying client 7

then reverted once it proved unnecessary — see below) instead lets the
*session's own quiescence logic* conclude the run is finished and shut the
whole server down (`accept failed: quic connection: endpoint closed`)

connected yet cannot be "mid-baseline" (`lj.any_joining()`) and so does not
count toward keeping the session alive. Both failure modes were reproduced
and confirmed directly (not inferred) before settling on the simpler,
uniform-delay fixture as the accurate, reproducible repro of the real
underlying limitation. The per-client connect-delay-override harness
addition explored while chasing the second failure mode did not end up
being needed by the shipped fixture and was reverted in full, per this
project's own "don't add code beyond what's needed" convention — not because

connection-level idle timeout, not a connect-timing problem) and would have
shipped as speculative, fixture-less code otherwise.

**This is a genuine, previously-unmeasured product limitation, not a fixture
bug**: the current implementation cannot reliably serve a worst-case-sized
dependency-complete baseline to a single network-constrained client while
several other simultaneous joiners are also capturing — exactly the

background-capture thread priority or parallelism headroom; or send an
early, cheap "capture in progress" control record so the connection has
genuine traffic (and a legitimate reason to extend the idle window) while
its capture is pending.

## Increment 27 — row 7, first slice: `ResidencyPass` gains real ack-before-evict — 2026-09-13

Picked up row 7's oldest open item — reconciling G3's `ResidencyPass` with
T18's more-rigorous `ResidencyController`/`ResidencyBacking` contract — as a
first, deliberately narrow slice rather than the full unification (own,
larger design pass; T18's `ResidencyController` is dormant library code today,
never wired into `serve.rs`, and merging the two APIs is a bigger change than
warranted for one increment).

**The gap, precisely:** `ResidencyController::enforce_budget` (T18,
`spall_server::residency`) persists a dirty eviction candidate to its
`ResidencyBacking` and only evicts it from the live cache if that `persist`
call actually succeeds (`?`-propagated — a backing error leaves the geometry
resident, never silently evicted). `ResidencyPass::run` (G3/T23) had no
equivalent gate: `spall_sim::BrickBacking` has no write method at all, and
`MemoryBacking::capture` was called out-of-band at commit time
(`ResidencyPass::on_commit`), *infallibly* (`let Ok(Some(snap)) = ... else {
return; };` swallowed any failure with no propagation), with eviction later
proceeding on interest/hysteresis alone — trusting an earlier capture had
already landed, never checking at the moment it actually mattered.

**Fix, scoped to close exactly that gap:** `MemoryBacking::capture` now
returns `bool` (`true` = the backing record now reflects the live brick;
`false` = nothing written, brick wasn't resident to snapshot). `ResidencyPass::run`'s
eviction loop calls a fresh `capture` immediately before every
`world.evict_brick` — gated: eviction only proceeds if that capture just
succeeded. A failed capture skips eviction for that tick; the brick stays
resident, and `waited` (already past `EVICT_SETTLE_TICKS`) stays elevated so
the very next tick retries — the same "persist-then-evict, gated" shape as
`enforce_budget`, without touching `ResidencyController` itself or requiring
`BrickBacking` to grow a full write API. `on_commit`'s existing capture is
unchanged (still useful — it's what makes reload/repair/checkpoint-read paths
current between evictions — just no longer the *only* thing standing between
an edit and a bad eviction).

Added `MemoryBacking::fail_next_capture(volume, coord)` — a one-shot,
`#[doc(hidden)] pub` test-only fault injector (matching this workspace's
`spall_store::inject` convention for cross-crate integration-test access, not
`#[cfg(test)]`, which doesn't reach another crate's integration tests): makes
the *next* `capture` call for that key fail, as if the brick had unexpectedly
stopped being resident, then reverts to normal.

## Increment 28 — row 7: backing-trait unification, a real disk-backed `BrickBacking`, and total-resident-memory evidence — 2026-09-17

Branch `feat/eng-30-t23-row7-residency-unification`, off `main`. Picks up all
three items increment 27 left open, in the priority order given: the
API-duplication reconciliation first (since items 2 and 3 build on it), then
the disk-backed store, then memory measurement.

### Design decision: a backing-trait bridge, not a full controller merge

Investigated exactly how the four mechanisms differ before touching anything.
`ResidencyController`/`ClientResidency` (T18, `spall_server::residency` /
`spall_client::residency`) and `ResidencyPass`/`ClientResidencyPass` (T23,
`spall_server::residency_pass` / `spall_client::residency`) are **not**
two implementations racing for the same call site — a repo-wide grep for
`ResidencyController::new` / `ClientResidency::new` finds them only inside
their own unit tests (`crates/spall_server/tests/residency.rs`,
`crates/spall_client/tests/residency.rs`). Neither is instantiated from
`serve()`, `spall_client::net`, or any binary. `ResidencyPass` (wired into
`serve.rs` since increment 9) and `ClientResidencyPass` (wired into
`spall_client::net` since increment 11) are the only mechanisms any gate
scenario or production binary ever runs. So the "four mechanisms" are two
live, shipped, scenario-proven passes and two dormant, tested-but-unused
generic libraries — a real duplication in the tree (two designs for one
concept), but not a live behavioral conflict today.

The T18 pair is also functionally richer than what `ResidencyPass` needs
today: `ResidencyCache` (`spall_voxel::residency`) is a genuine LRU with
per-brick dirty/durable/pin tracking and a dense-byte budget, `ResidencyController`
adds structural-dependency resolution (`resolve_structure`, following
`Support::Unknown` chains) and a `BodySpatialIndex` for partitioned dynamic
bodies — none of which `ResidencyPass` needs, because it only ever evicts a
single terrain volume by a simple interest box, and bodies are pinned
complete everywhere. Replacing `ResidencyPass`'s eviction bookkeeping with
`ResidencyCache` wholesale would be a genuine behavioral rewrite of the exact
code the last dozen increments' scenario hashes (`t23-g3-residency`,
`t23-g3-traversal`, the G4 workload/soak lanes) depend on — precisely the
kind of "rewrite a shared interface as a convenience" AGENTS.md warns against
without a standalone design pass, and increment 27 already reached the same
conclusion for the narrower ack-before-evict gate alone ("a bigger change
than warranted for one increment").

There is also a hard architectural reason the two backing traits cannot
literally *become* one type: `spall_sim::BrickBacking` (T23) is read-only and
`Brick`-typed on purpose, so `spall_sim` never depends on `spall_store` —
`docs/architecture.md`'s persistence section spells this out ("rather than
adding a `spall_sim -> spall_store` edge that would pull the vendored SQLite C
build into the otherwise pure simulation crate"). `spall_server::residency::ResidencyBacking`
(T18) is `StoredBrick`-typed and lives in `spall_server`, which already
depends on both. Moving `ResidencyController` down into `spall_sim` to unify
the trait literally would violate that boundary for a mechanism nothing
currently calls.

**What was reconciled, concretely:** the one seam that item 2 and item 3
actually needed — a shared *write-capable* backing contract — not the
eviction-policy engines themselves. `spall_sim::backing::BrickBackingWriter`
(new, `trait BrickBackingWriter: BrickBacking { fn capture(...) -> bool; fn
disk_bytes(&self) -> Option<u64> { None } }`) is the "ack-before-evict" half
of the row-7 contract, generalized from `MemoryBacking`'s existing inherent
`capture` method. Both `MemoryBacking` (in-process, historical default) and
the new `DiskBrickBacking` (item 2) implement it, so `ResidencyPass` —
`install`, `on_commit`, `run`'s eviction gate, `capture_checkpoint` — is
written once against the trait and does not know or care which is installed.
`ResidencyPass::install` is unchanged (still builds a `MemoryBacking`, byte-
identical to every prior run); a new `ResidencyPass::install_with_backing`
takes any `Arc<dyn BrickBackingWriter>`. `ResidencyPass::backing()` now
returns `Arc<dyn BrickBacking>` (an upcast — Rust 1.86+ trait-upcasting
coercion, this workspace pins 1.96.1) since the late-join/repair capture path
only ever reads.

`ResidencyController`/`ClientResidency` were left in place, not deleted:
deleting real, tested, non-trivial policy code (LRU + dense-byte budget +
collision-sweep readiness + structural resolution) with no replacement would
itself be "discarding existing work" for a future generalization (multi-
volume / larger-world residency, the kind of scope `docs/reports/ENG-31-t24-g5-feasibility.md`
scopes for G5) that this increment does not attempt. Cross-reference comments
were added at both `ResidencyController`'s and `ResidencyPass`'s module docs
pointing at this section, so the duplication reads as a documented,
reasoned-about state rather than an accidental one on the next pass.

**What was not attempted and is still open:** merging the eviction-policy
engines themselves (`ResidencyCache` vs. the interest-box+settle-tick logic);
adapting `ResidencyController`/`ClientResidency` to also use
`BrickBackingWriter` (`ResidencyBacking::persist` takes `&mut self` — a shape
mismatch with the shared `Arc<dyn BrickBackingWriter>` the live path needs,
and `ResidencyController` is unused in production so there is no live bug
this would fix); and any client-side disk backing (the task's own item 2
wording scopes this to the server / `sandbox-server` production path, and
`ClientResidencyPass` has no durable-backing concept at all today — it only
ever evicts and re-requests over the network).

### Item 2: a real disk-backed `BrickBacking`

`spall_store::residency_store::ResidencyStore` (new module) is a small,
separate SQLite database — same low-level conventions as the rest of
`spall_store` (WAL, zstd-compressed payloads via the existing
`encode_cells`/`decode_cells`/`BrickPayload`), but **deliberately not part of
the versioned save schema** (`STORE_SCHEMA_VERSION` / `dto.rs` / `schema.rs`
are untouched): it is a rebuildable cache the residency subsystem writes far
more often than a checkpoint (every commit that touches a resident brick, and
again, gated, immediately before every eviction), and — per row 7's own
framing — losing it costs nothing but a re-capture, since journal replay from
a checkpoint reconstructs the world independently of any residency backing.
Putting it in its own table in its own file means every write skips
`STORE_SCHEMA_VERSION`'s migration contract entirely; the module docs spell
out this reasoning. `crates/spall_server/src/disk_backing.rs`'s
`DiskBrickBacking` wraps a `ResidencyStore` and implements
`spall_sim::{BrickBacking, BrickBackingWriter}`, converting to/from
`BrickPayload` the same way `spall_server::persist`'s existing
`stored_brick_from_backing` does.

Wired into `sandbox-server` as `--residency-disk-backing` (only meaningful
with `--residency-budget-bricks > 0`), opening `<world>/residency.db`
alongside the existing `<world>/world.db`. `ServeConfig` gained
`residency_disk_path: Option<PathBuf>`; unset (the default, and every
existing `ServeConfig` literal in the tree updated to `None`), `serve()`
installs `MemoryBacking` exactly as before — byte-identical to every prior
run. A disk-open failure fails the run closed (`SimResult::error`) rather
than silently falling back to memory.

## Increment 29 — row 11: two real bugs found and fixed behind the stateless reset; the reset itself replaced by a sharper, deeper diagnosis (still open) — 2026-09-17

Picked up increment 26's open question directly: why does the server's
endpoint forget client 7's connection state entirely by ~33 s in, producing
`quinn::ConnectionError::Reset` ("reset by peer") with no `Bye`? Reproduced
first (`cargo xtask scenario --name t23-g4-join-budget --timeout-ms 300000`):
client 7 fails byte-for-byte as increment 26 described, confirmed directly
(temporary instrumentation, reverted after use, same convention as increment
26) rather than re-trusted from the prior report — `client7.jsonl` shows
failure at `elapsed=32.57s`; a temporary `Connection::debug_close_reason()`
accessor and an enriched client-side error message confirmed `close_reason =
Some("reset by peer")` on two more independent runs (`32.942s`, `32.967s`),
matching increment 26 exactly.

**Root cause #1, found (not guessed) via direct instrumentation and fixed.**
Temporary `eprintln!` diagnostics in `Connection::run_liveness` (both roles)
and around the server's background baseline-capture thread
(`LateJoin::on_baseline_ack` / `publish_ready_captures`) showed the SERVER's
own `run_liveness` task for client 7 logging `closing (idle timeout,
since_last_control=30.3578587s)` at connection-age `t=30.508s` — its own
watchdog, correctly applying `cfg.idle_timeout` (30 s, unwidened — this run
predates any of increment 26's reverted widenings), concluding client 7 had
gone silent. It had: `spall_client::net::run_async` (`crates/spall_client/src/net.rs`)
spawned the client's own `run_liveness` task — the *only* thing that sends
periodic application `Heartbeat`s — **after** `perform_late_join(...).await`
returned, not before. `perform_late_join` can legitimately block for tens of
seconds waiting on `BaselineBegin` (exactly the CPU-contended capture
increment 25 measured), and during that entire wait the client sends exactly
one control record (`BaselineAck{sentinel}`) and then nothing else. The
SERVER correctly measures 30+ seconds of genuine silence from client 7 and
closes the connection as idle — not a bug in the watchdog, a bug in never
arming the client's half of it. `crates/spall_net/src/harness.rs`'s own
`run_client`/`run_server_connection` already spawn `run_liveness` immediately
after connect, confirming this is the correct, established pattern the
sandbox client had drifted from. **Fix:** moved the `stop_tx`/`stop_rx`
channel and the `run_liveness` spawn in `run_async` to immediately after the
connection is authenticated, before `perform_late_join` (and before
`want_baseline` is even computed); the early-failure path now also stops and
aborts the task it starts. This explains why increment 26's *client-side*
idle-watchdog widening (its instrumentation step 1) made no difference: it
widened the tolerance of a watchdog that would not exist yet during the wait
it was trying to protect.

**Root cause #2, found the same way once #1 was fixed.** With #1 alone,
`t23-g4-join-budget` still failed, now much later (server-side
`run_liveness` logging `closing (heartbeat write timeout, write pending for
500.9ms, ctrl_send locked=true)` at `t=72.5s`). The heartbeat write's own
timeout (`tokio::time::timeout(self.cfg.heartbeat_interval, write)`, i.e.
500 ms) is tighter than it needs to be: a `Heartbeat` write shares its
connection's congestion/flow-control budget with real application traffic on
the same control stream (in particular the small `BaselineBegin`/
`BaselineEnd` records `send_baseline` writes around a client's bulk
transfer). On client 7's bandwidth-capped (1 MiB/s), lossy (2%) link, that
write can legitimately still be pending well past 500 ms without the peer
being unresponsive — the watchdog's own better answer to "how long is
genuine unresponsiveness tolerated," `idle_timeout` (30 s), already exists
and is more appropriate here than a value tied to the heartbeat cadence.
**Fix:** `crates/spall_net/src/conn.rs`'s `run_liveness` now bounds the
heartbeat write by `self.cfg.idle_timeout` instead of
`self.cfg.heartbeat_interval` — a write that is merely contending with real
traffic is never treated as a dead peer sooner than genuine silence would be.

**Both fixes are real, are independently justified (not workarounds tied to
this one adversarial scenario), and are kept even though they do not fully
close row 11** (below): fix #1 removes a genuine spurious-disconnect risk for
*any* late join whose baseline capture runs long under load, worst-case-sized
scene or not; fix #2 removes a needlessly tight internal bound that had no
correctness purpose once `idle_timeout` already exists to catch a truly dead
peer. Verified clean re-runs of `t23-g4-join-budget` after each fix showed
the failure's mechanism and timing changing exactly as predicted at each
step, which is itself confirmation the diagnosis was right at each stage, not
a coincidence.

**Row 11 is still not closed.** With both fixes applied and all temporary
diagnostics reverted, `t23-g4-join-budget` fails deterministically and
consistently at `elapsed=52.00s` / `52.01s` across repeated runs (`client7.jsonl`:
`"connection closed before the baseline arrived: quic stream: connection
lost"`) — no crash, no panic (confirmed absent from captured process output),
and (per the instrumented runs immediately before the final revert) still a
watchdog closing on **genuine, measured silence in both directions for over
30 real seconds** — not a false read of an idle connection this time, an
actually-idle one. This is a sharper, deeper diagnosis than increment 26 left:
the server's own tiny 500 ms-serializable heartbeat literally cannot be
gotten out the door for 30+ seconds under this scenario's combination of (a)
eight concurrent raw-OS-thread background baseline captures competing for
real CPU on the same machine as the server's and every client's own tokio
runtime, and (b) client 7's connection additionally being bandwidth-capped
and lossy. Individually measured capture-thread durations were unremarkable
(~1.5-2.3 s of wall time each, per-thread) — the problem is not any one
capture being slow, it is eight of them plus nine-plus OS processes (one
server, eight clients) all wanting CPU at once on this machine long enough to
starve *literally everything* (including client processes' own ability to
poll their sockets) for 30+ seconds at a stretch. Notably, `std::thread::
available_parallelism()` reports 16 logical cores on this environment — eight
CPU-bound threads should not, on paper, starve a 16-thread-capable box this
badly; either real usable/schedulable CPU here is well below what the OS
reports (a sandboxed/virtualized agent environment's quota, not host
hardware, would explain that gap), or the starvation has a compounding
element not yet isolated (e.g. the raw `std::thread::spawn` captures are
never bounded in count and could in principle pile up beyond eight if a
retry/re-capture path fires during the same window — not observed here, but
not yet ruled out either).

**Deliberately not attempted this increment:** bounding the number of
concurrent background capture threads (e.g. a small fixed worker pool instead
of one `std::thread::spawn` per requesting client). This is the obvious next
lever and is plausibly the real fix, but picking a pool size without first
establishing this environment's actual usable CPU budget (see above) would be
exactly the kind of unprincipled, environment-fitted guess this project's
convention (and increment 26's own precedent) asks not to ship. The right
next step is measuring that budget first, not tuning a number to make one
sandboxed run pass.

Files changed: `crates/spall_client/src/net.rs` (`run_liveness` spawn moved
before `perform_late_join`; early-failure path stops/aborts it; the discarded
transport error on the `BaselineBegin` wait's `Err` arm is now included in
the reported message), `crates/spall_net/src/conn.rs` (heartbeat write now
bounded by `idle_timeout`, not `heartbeat_interval`). No fixture or protocol
changes. Verified: `cargo xtask scenario --name t23-g4-join-budget
--timeout-ms 300000` still `session FAILED` / `requirements_met: false`
(row 11 remains an accurate failing repro, now for a different, better-
understood reason); `cargo fmt --all --check` clean; `cargo clippy --workspace
--all-targets --all-features -- -D warnings` clean; `cargo test --workspace
--all-features` — full 79-binary suite, zero failures on a clean run
(`spall_net::separate_process_transport` failed once on an earlier run in
this session and passed on immediate re-run, consistent with its known,
unrelated flake — not a regression from either fix here).

Status remains **in_progress**. Next unblocked: measure this environment's
actual schedulable CPU budget under the g4-workload's 8-way concurrent
capture load (before sizing any capture-concurrency bound), or row 7's
still-open eviction-policy-engine merge (`ResidencyCache`/`ResidencyController`
vs. the interest-box passes — deliberately deferred in increment 28) — no

## Increment 30 — row 11: measured this environment's real CPU budget, shipped a bounded background-capture worker pool on its own merits, but it does not close row 11 (a sharper, still-open lead found instead) — 2026-09-17

schedulable CPU budget under concurrent load *before* sizing any bounded
worker pool for `LateJoin::on_baseline_ack`'s background baseline captures —

constants" convention (increment 26's own precedent).

the exact unbounded thread-per-request pattern under test), swept at

**Finding 1: the reported 16 logical cores are real and genuinely
schedulable in isolation.** Throughput scales smoothly (with gracefully
degrading per-worker efficiency: +12% at N=2 up to +48% at N=16) right up to
N = 16 — exactly `available_parallelism()` — then flatlines/degrades purely
from queueing beyond it (N=24..48 all sit at ~5.2 workers/s regardless).
There is no early cliff and no hidden throttle. This directly answers
increment 29's open question ("is the reported core count even real here?")
in this isolated single-process probe: yes. The severe real-world starvation
increment 29 measured (a single tiny heartbeat write blocked for 30+s under
only eight capture threads) is therefore not explained by a fake/virtualized
core count.

**Finding 2 (source-inspection, not live-measured): a much larger,
un-isolated multiplier exists in this workspace's actual multi-process
shape.** `t23-g4-join-budget` runs nine separate OS processes (one
`sandbox-server`, eight `sandbox-client`s — confirmed via
`tools/xtask/src/session.rs`'s `Command::new(...)` call sites, not a shared
process). Every one of them builds its own Tokio runtime via
`tokio::runtime::Builder::new_multi_thread()` — `crates/spall_server/src/serve.rs`'s
`serve()`, `crates/spall_client/src/net.rs`'s `run_async()`, and even
`xtask`'s own harness-side connections in `session.rs` — and **none of them
calls `.worker_threads(..)`**, so every one defaults to
`available_parallelism()` (`16` here). That puts up to `9 x 16 = 144` OS
threads on this machine's 16 physical/logical cores *before a single capture
thread is ever spawned* — an oversubscription factor the isolated
single-process probe above cannot see or measure, and a plausible
explanation for why eight additional capture threads (a small fraction of
that ~144) were enough to starve everything for 30+ real seconds despite 16
genuinely-real cores. This was **not** confirmed by live process/thread
sampling — an attempt to sample `sandbox-server.exe` / `sandbox-client.exe`
thread counts during a real run via Windows `tasklist` polling every 3-4s
missed every process instance (a tooling/timing gap, not a negative result:
the scenario's live processes exist only for the run's duration and the
poll cadence was too coarse) — so this is flagged as a specific, falsifiable
next lead, not a proven root cause.

**Decision: implement the bounded pool anyway, on its own merits, sized
conservatively off the measured curve.** An unbounded thread-per-request
spawn is a known scalability antipattern independent of any one sandbox's
contention (per this ticket's own framing), so it is worth fixing regardless
of whether it turns out to be *the* fix for `t23-g4-join-budget`. Per-worker
latency overhead from the table above is mild through a quarter of
`available_parallelism()` (+21% at N=4) and climbs steadily worse from there
(+29% at half, +48% at the full count) — and because background capture must
additionally share this machine with the much larger, un-isolated
multi-process Tokio thread population from Finding 2, the default was set to
the conservative quarter-of-parallelism point rather than the fuller
"still efficient" N=8–16 range the isolated probe alone would suggest:
`default_capture_workers() = (available_parallelism() / 4).max(1)` — `4` on
this environment. This is a measured, host-derived default (via
`std::thread::available_parallelism()` at runtime), not a literal picked by
trial-and-error against this one scenario.

Rayon was checked first per this ticket's instructions ("check if rayon has
a suitable primitive... before hand-rolling one"). It is **not** actually a
workspace dependency — `README.md` lists it only as a design-inspiration
reference alongside winit/hecs/serde/Jolt, and `cargo tree -i rayon` finds no
such package anywhere in the dependency graph. Adding it as a new real
dependency of `spall_server` for one bounded-queue primitive would cut
against this project's minimal-dependency convention for a few dozen lines
of code, so a hand-rolled pool was used instead (matching this ticket's own
"this workspace doesn't need a crate for this" framing).

**Implementation.** `crates/spall_server/src/serve.rs`: a new `CapturePool`
(a `std::sync::mpsc` job channel shared by `workers` long-lived OS threads
behind an `Arc<Mutex<Receiver<_>>>`) replaces the raw `std::thread::spawn`

`capture_pool: CapturePool` field constructed in `LateJoin::new` from a new
third `capture_workers: usize` parameter. `ServeConfig` gains a
`capture_workers: usize` field (default `default_capture_workers()`, a new

`max_join_retries`. `sandbox-server` gets an optional `--capture-workers`
CLI flag (`Option<usize>`, `None` → the measured default) following the same
pattern as `--catch-up-cap`. `sandbox-capture.rs` and the three
`ServeConfig`-literal integration tests
(`crates/spall_server/tests/{interest_bandwidth,late_join_session,replication_session}.rs`)

(`serve::tests::capture_pool_bounds_concurrent_execution_to_its_worker_count`,

**`t23-g4-join-budget` outcome: the pool does not close row 11.** Three

## Increment 34 — row 7: incremental (non-full-reload) checkpoint capture

Date: 2026-09-18. Stacked on increment 33. Closes the other item
`docs/reports/ENG-30-row7-remaining.md`'s "preserve delivered behavior"
section named as still open alongside durable exact-revision acknowledgement:
"checkpoint capture without reloading the live cache have landed" (increment
22) is not the same claim as *incremental* capture, and `docs/tasks.md`
increment 13's own deferred list named "incremental (non-full-reload)
checkpoint capture" as distinct from that already-delivered bounded capture.

**The gap.** Increment 22 stopped `ResidencyPass::capture_checkpoint` from
`reload_all`-ing every evicted brick back into the live world before a
checkpoint — memory no longer spiked to the full world at checkpoint time.
But the capture itself was still a full logical-world walk every single call:
every currently-resident terrain brick was freshly re-snapshotted and
zstd-encoded (`persist::stored_bricks`), and every currently-evicted brick was
freshly reloaded from the backing and encoded, regardless of whether either
had changed since the previous checkpoint. Under a tight residency budget most
terrain is evicted most of the time, so the evicted-brick loop alone repeated
this full backing-read-plus-encode cost on every periodic and shutdown
checkpoint even for bricks nobody had touched in the whole run.

**The fix.** `ResidencyPass` gains a `checkpoint_cache:
BTreeMap<BrickCoord, (Revision, StoredBrick)>` — the last checkpoint that
actually captured each terrain coordinate's encoded record. It is seeded at
install time from every then-resident brick, in the same walk that already
seeds the backing (`install_with_backing`), so a run's *first* real checkpoint
already benefits, not only later ones comparing against an earlier real
checkpoint. On each `capture_checkpoint` call: for a resident brick, its
current live revision (`Volume::snapshot_brick`) is compared against the
cache; for an evicted brick, its retained digest's revision is compared. A
match reuses the prior `StoredBrick` record verbatim — no live snapshot / no
backing load / no zstd re-encode. A miss (an edit bumped the revision, or the
coordinate was never cached) does the real work exactly as before and updates
the cache. Revision equality implying content equality is not a new
assumption here — it is the same one `EvictedBricks::verify_reload` and the
rest of the digest lifecycle already rely on
(`docs/reports/G3-residency-hash.md`'s digest lifecycle table).

Every checkpoint's `bricks` list is still the **complete** logical (resident ∪
evicted) terrain set — checkpoints are pruned (`RETAIN_CHECKPOINTS` in
`serve.rs`) and each one must stand alone for a cold recovery, so this is not
a differential/delta checkpoint format. Only how that complete list is
*assembled* changed: reused records for unchanged bricks, freshly captured
records for dirty ones. A capture that fails partway (an evicted brick with no
durable backing record, `PersistError::EvictedBrickUnavailable`, unchanged
from increment 22) leaves the cache and its counters untouched — the same
fail-closed convention as the rest of this pass.

`spall_server::persist::capture` is refactored, not behaviourally changed: its
body is now a thin wrapper — `stored_bricks(&terrain.volume)` then a call to a
new `pub(crate) capture_with_terrain_bricks(sim, cfg, cursor, terrain_bricks)`
that does everything else (bodies, meta, world hash) exactly as before.
`ResidencyPass::capture_checkpoint` calls `capture_with_terrain_bricks`
directly with its incrementally-assembled terrain-brick list instead of

## Increment 36 — row 7: durable exact-revision backing acknowledgement, audit then fix

Date: 2026-09-18. Closes the second item increment 33's and 34's "Not done"
lists both named: "durable exact-revision acknowledgement (still an
in-memory/disk ack-before-evict contract, not a two-phase durable commit)."
Per `docs/reports/ENG-30-row7-remaining.md`'s explicit caution — "a boolean
synchronous capture result is not by itself proof that exact-revision
acknowledgement is missing; any API replacement needs a demonstrated need" —
this increment audits the actual call paths before changing anything, rather
than assuming the deferred-list wording meant the feature was simply absent.

**Audit, boundary by boundary.** The frozen contract's Evict row requires:
"On the server, require a durable ack of *that exact revision* first. Publish
the retained digest and drop the geometry together, on the owning thread. A
failure leaves geometry available." Tracing `ResidencyPass::run`'s evict call
site: it calls `backing.capture(...)` and then `world.evict_brick(...)` on
the same tick, same thread, against the same `&mut SimWorld` borrow — the
borrow checker itself rules out any interleaving between the two, for both
`MemoryBacking` and `DiskBrickBacking`. **This boundary was already correct;
no change made.** Separately, `residency.db`'s `synchronous=NORMAL` pragma
looked like a candidate durability gap, but tracing `spall_store::recover` /
`recover_conn_from` confirms crash recovery reads only `world.db`'s
checkpoint + journal and never touches `residency.db` — so that pragma is not
load-bearing for any acknowledgement the contract requires, and was left
alone rather than tightened without a demonstrated need.

**The real gap, found by tracing the checkpoint path instead.**
`ResidencyPass::capture_checkpoint` read evicted-brick records straight from
the backing into the **durable** (`synchronous=FULL`) checkpoint with no
verification against the retained digest — unlike `SimWorld::reload_brick`,
which validates every candidate via `EvictedBricks::verify_candidate` before
ever letting it back into the live world. A stale or corrupted backing record
for an evicted brick could silently reach the durable save file while
`checkpoint.world_hash` kept reporting the (still-correct, resident-only)
value — a genuine, if narrow, gap in the contract's "capture ... from the
current snapshot" guarantee.

**The fix.** `capture_checkpoint` now verifies each evicted brick's backing
record against its retained `BrickDigest` via the same `verify_candidate`
machinery `reload_brick` already uses — no new API shape, no two-phase
commit protocol invented. A mismatch returns a new
`PersistError::EvictedBrickDigestMismatch { volume, coord, retained_revision,
backing_revision, .. }` and fails the whole capture closed, leaving the prior
checkpoint and the live/evicted geometry untouched — the same fail-closed
convention the rest of this pass already follows.

**Evidence.** New fault-injection test
`capture_checkpoint_fails_closed_on_a_backing_record_that_disagrees_with_the_retained_digest`
in `crates/spall_server/tests/residency_pass.rs`: confirmed failing against
the pre-fix code (a forged backing record was silently accepted, with forged
bytes reaching `checkpoint.bricks`) and passing post-fix (capture rejects the
mismatch and returns `EvictedBrickDigestMismatch` naming the exact brick and
both revisions). `cargo test -p spall_server --test residency_pass` (12/12).
`cargo xtask scenario --name t23-g3-residency` and `t23-g3-residency-disk`
converge to the established agreed hash
`cac2893cf5e76d866c9c1111b97fe0f822a86e9956f56f3d3ee3a8740d18e6de`, unchanged.
`cargo xtask scenario --name t23-g3-traversal` converges to
`2a47eda7e3b9c7c17446dfe5e94ab9fd961fb0d1754eba5f5321fec7bb3b51d8`, unchanged.
`cargo xtask crash-test --suite persistence` (12/12); `cargo test -p

### Root causes (with the data that shows them)

1. **Replica divergence under impairment = motion datagrams starving the reliable
   topology stream.** Timelines (`.local/runs/g4i-normal-timeline2`): the server
   handed transaction #500 to the transport at 154 s; the client received it at
   230 s (76 s behind), and clients were cut off at shutdown with 425-1,008 of
   1,213. QUIC samples: `cwnd` 3-8 KB at 100 ms/2% loss (Mathis-model ceiling
   ~45 KB/s), i.e. less than the ~50+ KB/s of motion offered, so unsent reliable
   bytes queue *inside quinn*, where the application backlog metric cannot see
   them. Causality: with the motion budget cut to 1,000 B/batch every joined client
   received all 1,063 transactions with matching hashes (`g4diag-lowmotion`).
   **Fix:** `MotionInterest::congestion_aware` (`--motion-congestion-aware`) caps
   each client's motion at 25% of measured `cwnd/rtt`. My earlier "reliable
   backlog recovered within 5 s" PASS measured the wrong queue; it is renamed
   "application send-queue" and a client-observed end-to-end topology-lag check was
   added.
2. **Simultaneous joins failing = handshakes serialised in one accept loop.**
   `accept_log`: each `Server::accept()` (QUIC handshake + authentication) took
   1-2.8 s under 100 ms/2%, one at a time, so the 8th joiner accepted at ~9.7 s,
   past the 5 s client handshake timeout (3-4 of 8 failed; 2 of 8 at 200 ms/5%
   even with a 20 s override). **Fix:** 8 concurrent accept loops; all 8 accepted in
   1.7 s with the *default* 5 s timeout. The 20 s `SPALL_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT_MS` knob
   is diagnostic only and is now used by no gate scenario.
3. **Join readiness 35-41 s.** Timeline decomposition on the server clock: scene
   build 16.6 s + initial checkpoint capture 2.2 s (both before the first tick;
   clients wait through them) + `snapshot_world` on the tick thread **~2.0 s per
   joiner, serialised (8 x 2 s = 16 s)** + worker encode 1.65 s + client
   receive/decode/install/ack ~5 s. **Fix:** joiners that ask while a capture at
   the same journal cursor is in flight share it (one snapshot, one encode;
   unit-tested). Readiness fell to **27.3-29.0 s** (100 ms/2%) and **27.1-29.8 s**
   (200 ms/5%): under 30 s but with ~1 s margin, dominated by the 16.6 s scene
   build.
4. **30-minute soak growth.** Bodies grow **exactly one per edit** (8,866 commits
   -> +8,865 bodies): each comb-tooth cut detaches one rubble body, each blast one
   segment. The census (`--ignored rubble_census`) shows why they never leave the
   solver: after 90 s, **311 of 1,102 awake rubble bodies are at y down to -16 km,
   falling at 400 m/s** — small fast rods **tunnel through the 1 m ground slab**
   (CCD is disabled server-wide) and free-fall forever, awake, replicated and
   journaled; most of the rest sit awake near the ground and sleep slowly. In the
   soak, solver-awake bodies climbed 255 -> 9,194. Durable growth follows: the
   journal writes a `PoseBatch` of every non-resting body every motion batch
   (19,985 batches + 8,866 topology records = the 28,851 records seen; 363 KB per
   write average), so `world.db` reached 8.9 GB. Any fix must keep solid matter
   and durable recovery (body lifecycle: stop the tunnelling with a thicker or
